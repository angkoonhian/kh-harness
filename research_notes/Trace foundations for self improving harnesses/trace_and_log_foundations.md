# Trace and log foundations for self-improving harnesses (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-18. This file extends `Self improving agents and harnesses/agent_self_improvement_science.md` and does not repeat its headline numbers, selection rules or safety findings. Method notes for the report writer:

- Every figure below was read from the cited page in this session unless a bullet says "abstract only", "search snippet" or "secondary".
- Pages were read through a fetch tool that returns extracted passages. Quotation marks mean the passage was returned as verbatim; long quotes may be clipped mid-sentence. Where the tool could only read the first 100,000 characters of a paper, the bullet says which part was not read.
- Publication dates are given at first mention. Where only a revision date was visible, it is labelled as such.
- Sources published before 2025 are tagged **[pre-2025]**.

## 1. Which papers and systems state that complete execution traces are the prerequisite for self-improvement, and is "log everything first" the consensus?

### Takeaway
"Keep the raw execution record and let the improver read it" is stated outright by Meta-Harness ("Log everything in a navigable format"), by the Microsoft Trace paper (traces "are akin to back-propagated gradients"), by GEPA's 2026 successor (side information "is the text-optimization analogue of the gradient") and by LangChain's practice posts (tracing is "the primitive that makes the entire improvement loop possible"). It is the consensus for what to *store*. It is not the consensus for what to *show the improver*: five 2026 papers put a structured diagnosis layer between the raw log and the proposer, and three of them report beating a raw-trace baseline.

### Cited Findings

**Explicit statements that traces are the foundation**
- Meta-Harness (Lee, Nair, Zhang, Lee, Khattab, Finn; submitted 30 March 2026), Appendix D, lists as an implementation tip: "Log everything in a navigable format", meaning machine-readable formats such as JSON, hierarchical organisation of artefacts, and consistent file names so that regex search works. The tool returned the tip's heading as verbatim and the rest as paraphrase — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- Meta-Harness, Table 3 caption: "Access to raw execution traces is the key ingredient for enabling harness search." Section 1: "Compressed feedback often removes the information needed to trace downstream failures to earlier harness decisions." — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- The Meta-Harness repository's onboarding guide (undated; repository footer 2026) instructs: "Save candidate source, evaluation configuration, scores, and execution traces, including errors and costs when available", "Associate every result with the exact candidate that produced it", "Start with ordinary files and directories", and "Give it filesystem access to all search artifacts, including prior candidate code, scores, and full execution traces." — [meta-harness ONBOARDING.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness/main/ONBOARDING.md)
- Trace / OptoPrime **[pre-2025]** (Cheng, Nie, Swaminathan; Microsoft Research and Stanford; arXiv ID June 2024, v2 dated 1 November 2024), abstract: "We discover that workflow execution traces are akin to back-propagated gradients in AutoDiff." Section 1.1: traces "provide the information needed to relate rich feedback to the parameters in general workflows." — [arXiv 2406.16218](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16218)
- GEPA `optimize_anything` (Agrawal, Lee, and 11 others including Khattab, Dimakis, Zaharia; blog dated 18 February 2026): "optimize_anything makes diagnostic feedback a first-class part of the evaluator contract"; "ASI is the text-optimization analogue of the gradient"; "ASI tells an LLM proposer why a candidate failed and how to fix it." Actionable Side Information may be "open-ended text, structured data, multi-objectives (through `scores`)" "or even images", that is, "anything that would help an expert understand the artifact and diagnose failures" — [GEPA blog](https://gepa-ai.github.io/gepa/blog/2026/02/18/introducing-optimize-anything/)
- GEPA paper (v1 25 July 2025), Section 1: the method uses signals such as "compiler error messages, before they are collapsed into scalar rewards"; "Because such serialized trajectories can be readily understood by modern LLMs"; Section 3.2 says execution and evaluation traces "provide substantial diagnostic value" — [arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v1)
- LangChain, "The agent improvement loop starts with a trace" (Sam Crowder, 31 March 2026): "In software, the code documents the app; in AI, the traces do"; the loop "begins with tracing and returns to tracing"; tracing is "the primitive that makes the entire improvement loop possible". It adds that a coding agent without trace data "makes changes based on incomplete information" — [LangChain blog](https://www.langchain.com/blog/traces-start-agent-improvement-loop)
- LangChain, "Improving Deep Agents with harness engineering" (Vivek Trivedy, 17 February 2026): "At LangChain, we use Traces to understand agent failure modes at scale." Their Trace Analyzer Skill is: "Fetch experiment traces from LangSmith", then "Spawn parallel error analysis agents → main agent synthesizes findings + suggestions", then "Aggregate feedback and make targeted changes to the harness." Self-reported result: deepagents-cli from 52.8 to 66.5 on Terminal Bench 2.0 with the model fixed — [LangChain blog](https://www.langchain.com/blog/improving-deep-agents-with-harness-engineering)
- ADAS **[pre-2025]** (Hu, Lu, Clune; 15 August 2024) is the negative case that proves the point: Section 6 says "we simply evaluate discovered agents on the evaluation set and use the numerical performance results" and proposes as future work to "enable the meta agent to analyze detailed running logs during the evaluation" — [arXiv 2408.08435](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435)
- Self-Harness (Shanghai AI Laboratory; v2 dated 12 August 2026) states the protocol "depends on the quality of verifier outcomes and trace records" and that self-improvement should be "grounded in behavioral evidence" — [arXiv 2606.09498](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09498v2)
- Trace-to-Architecture, T2A (UPenn, HIT Shenzhen, DP Technology; dated 12 October 2026): "Task outcomes alone provide limited guidance for this decision"; "Aggregate task scores alone therefore cannot establish whether a revision alleviates its targeted problem"; "a score decrease alone does not identify its specific cause" — [arXiv 2610.16725](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)
- DGM (blog 30 May 2025): each selected parent "analyzes its own benchmark evaluation logs, proposes the next feature to implement, and receives this proposal as a problem statement to execute" — [arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v2)

**Systems that store raw traces but deliberately do not hand them raw to the proposer (2026)**
- Self-Harness: the proposer sees "structured cross-case evidence rather than raw execution logs". Each failed trace gets a failure signature (terminal verifier cause, causal status of the agent behaviour, abstract agent mechanism); failures are grouped by exact signature match — [arXiv 2606.09498](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09498v2)
- HarnessFix (Institute of Software, Chinese Academy of Sciences; v2 dated 2 July 2026) argues raw traces are hard to diagnose because they are "language-heavy, reasoning-driven, and non-deterministic", and that "execution traces capture runtime behavior, whereas harness implementations are defined in static artifacts", so the two must be aligned first. It builds a Harness-aware Trace Intermediate Representation (HTIR) — [arXiv 2606.06324](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06324v2)
- EnactOpt (Tsinghua, Peking University, Kling AI, UIUC; dated 10 October 2026): the optimiser gets the source harness plus per-decision diagnoses and an episode summary; step references "do not trigger raw-log retrieval" and "Raw logs remain available locally for inspection, outside the optimizer's feedback interface." — [arXiv 2610.14977](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14977)
- RHO, "Evolving Agents in the Dark" (City University of Hong Kong and Microsoft Research Asia; v3 29 August 2026): trajectories pass through self-validation and self-consistency diagnosis that emit structured JSON with a 0 to 1 severity score before the proposal step — [arXiv 2606.05922](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)
- T2A: scripts group failed trajectories "using observable execution features", the development agent examines representative trajectories from each group, and every losing task must be assigned to a diagnosis cluster or marked unresolved — [arXiv 2610.16725](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)

**2026 work specifically about trace-driven harness improvement (new since round one)**
- AutoSaddler (Microsoft, KAIST, SUSTech, POSTECH; submitted 24 August 2026): a Diagnosis-Patch Agent built on the Claude Agent SDK receives "each failure trace" with the harness codebase and "structured guidance that enables it to progressively retrieve relevant trace details"; minibatches include "both successful and failed runs" — [arXiv 2608.23041](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23041v1)
- EMHO (Korea University, University of Arkansas, UW-Madison; dated 6 October 2026): retains experience traces from all iterations, including rejected candidates, and gives the optimiser the complete experience and harness histories — [arXiv 2610.08432](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08432)
- VeRO (Scale AI; arXiv ID February 2026, v4 dated 2 June 2026): a harness for optimiser agents with Git auto-commit snapshots of every file change, an Experiment Database of per-sample scores, errors and rollouts, a budget-gated runner, and a dataset abstraction that blocks the optimiser from test data — [arXiv 2602.22480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22480)
- "Trial by Trace" (TraceLite AI with CAS, Tsinghua and others; dated October 2026): a benchmark that scores harnesses from normalised traces; "31.1% of runs achieve task success despite failing at least one harness criterion" across 3,420 runs — [arXiv 2610.32285](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32285)

### Inferences
- The sources separate into two claims that are easy to conflate. Claim A, "record the complete run", is uncontested: every system that reports a trace-related result either stores full traces or names their absence as a limitation (ADAS). Claim B, "the improver should read raw traces", is contested in 2026.
- Even the systems that feed a digest keep the raw log and point back into it (EnactOpt's step references, EMHO's event IDs, HTIR's `raw`-anchored steps, Trial by Trace's `raw_event`). A local harness can therefore treat the raw log as the system of record and treat any digest as a derived, rebuildable view.
- Practitioner sources (LangChain) are vendor-authored and promote a hosted trace product; their statements agree with the papers but should be weighed as vendor claims.

### Gaps
- No source read uses the literal phrase "log everything first" as a staged-adoption recommendation (instrument before building any improver). Meta-Harness's tip is the closest and it is an implementation tip, not a sequencing claim.
- I found no statement from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google DeepMind in this session that names traces as the prerequisite for harness self-improvement.
- AlphaEvolve, Reflexion, ExpeL, Voyager, TextGrad, HGM and Hyperagents do not state a traces-as-prerequisite position in the portions read; what they record is in section 2.

## 2. For each system: what is captured per step, in what format, how long it is kept, and how does the improving component read it?

### Takeaway
Three reading patterns exist: (i) a fixed prompt template filled with one task's log (DGM, GEPA, Trace, TextGrad, Reflexion), (ii) a sampled or retrieved slice of a database (AlphaEvolve/OpenEvolve, ExpeL, ADAS, SICA's archive tools), and (iii) an agent with file tools navigating an on-disk archive (Meta-Harness, AutoSaddler, VeRO, T2A). Most papers describe trace *content* in one sentence and give no schema; the schemas that exist are in repositories and in the 2026 diagnosis papers.

### Cited Findings

**Meta-Harness (30 March 2026)**
- Stored per candidate: "Each evaluated harness contributes a directory containing its source code, scores, and execution traces" "(such as prompts, tool calls, model outputs, and state updates)". Figure 2 caption: "All logs (proposed code, reasoning traces, evaluation scores) are stored in the filesystem in a new directory" — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- Read path: "the proposer queries it through terminal tools such as grep and cat rather than ingesting it as a single prompt"; "The filesystem is typically far larger than the proposer's context window" — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- Retention: all prior candidates are kept for the run (about 60 harnesses over 20 iterations); the proposer references "over 20 prior candidates per step" in the median setting — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- Appendix D adds an optional CLI over the logs (list the Pareto frontier, show top harnesses, diff code and results between runs), and says offline experience can be converted into the same directory structure to warm-start a search (paraphrase returned by the fetch tool) — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- The paper gives no directory tree or trace schema; the onboarding guide says "Start with ordinary files and directories" and names only `domain_spec.md` — [meta-harness ONBOARDING.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness/main/ONBOARDING.md)

**Darwin Gödel Machine (May 2025)**
- The diagnosis prompt (Appendix C.3) is a template with six slots: the agent's implementation (`{code}`), the agent's running log (`{md_log}`), the GitHub issue (`{github_issue}`), the agent's predicted patch (`{predicted_patch}`), SWE-bench's private test patch (`{test_patch}`, which the agent cannot see during evaluation) and the issue test results (`{eval_log}`) — [arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v2)
- Archive: holds all agents that "compile successfully and retain the ability to edit a given codebase", with parent-child edges and per-node scores (Figure 3) — [arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v2)
- On disk: the README says "By default, outputs will be saved in the `output_dgm/` directory"; the repository ships `initial/` ("SWE-bench logs and performance of the initial agent"), and a Google Drive folder that "contains all the foundation model output logs from the experiments shown in the paper" — [DGM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jennyzzt/dgm/main/README.md)
- Truncation: the only clipping described in the portion read is in the editor tool, where long output is "marked with `<response clipped>`" — [arXiv 2505.22954](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v2)

**Huxley-Gödel Machine (24 October 2025)**
- Tracks per node "the number of passed tests" and "the number of failed tests"; the archive "is always represented as a tree of evolved agents"; agents write self-modifications to diff patch files "usually with a constant file name" — [arXiv 2510.21614](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21614v3)
- The portion read does not describe what logs a child-creating agent receives.

**SICA (v2 16 May 2025)**
- Recorded: the "'callgraph' of agent and sub-agent execution, in addition to the event stream of LLM messages, tool calls and responses"; the execution tree records per-agent duration, tokens (with cache percentage), cost and status, with per-event timestamps — [arXiv 2504.15228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)
- Read path: the meta-agent is "instructed to look through the archive, identify an improvement, and implement it" through archive tools (`compare_agent_iterations`, `best_problems`, `worst_problems`); an archive-explorer sub-agent examines "the worst performing benchmark problems at a given iteration to understand the failure modes". The "archive analysis" tool "facilitates extracting high-level summary statistics about each agent iteration" — [arXiv 2504.15228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)
- The overseer reads "a comprehensive textual representation of the state of the system" every 30 s by default — [arXiv 2504.15228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)
- Retention: failed iterations stay ("While the failed iteration persists in the archive, in principle acting as an example of what not to do...") — [arXiv 2504.15228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)

**ADAS [pre-2025]**
- Archive entry: agent code plus evaluation metrics ("the agent is added to the archive along with its evaluation metrics"); the prompt carries a "Discovered Agent Archive" that is "initialized with baselines, updated at every iteration". No execution traces reach the meta agent. Errors during evaluation trigger a self-reflection step, retried "up to five times" — [arXiv 2408.08435](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435)

**AlphaEvolve (paper 16 June 2025) and OpenEvolve**
- AlphaEvolve "continually generates a growing number of solutions with evaluation results (scores and program outputs) attached to them"; the evaluator returns "a dictionary of scalars". Prompts contain "multiple previously discovered solutions sampled from the program database", optionally with rendered evaluation results (program, execution result, scores) — [arXiv 2506.13131](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)
- OpenEvolve defaults (file read 2026-11-18, undated): `checkpoint_interval: 10`; `log_prompts: true` ("log all prompts and responses into the database"); `db_path: null` (in-memory only unless set); `population_size: 1000`; `archive_size: 100`; `num_islands: 5`; `include_artifacts: true`; `max_artifact_bytes: 20480` ("20KB default"); `artifact_security_filter: true`; prompts include `num_top_programs: 3` and `num_diverse_programs: 2` — [OpenEvolve default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)
- OpenEvolve's optional `evolution_trace` is off by default (`enabled: false`); format `'jsonl'`, `'json'` or `'hdf5'`; `include_code: false`; `include_prompts: true`; `buffer_size: 10`; `compress: false`. The docs add `include_failures: true` to "Also log failed iterations to <trace name>_failures.jsonl" — [default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml); [OpenEvolve configuration docs](https://github.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/blob/main/docs/configuration.md)

**GEPA (July 2025) and optimize_anything (February 2026)**
- The feedback function "identifies relevant textual traces produced during the evaluation metric's execution" and "returns the final score along with feedback_text". Execution traces are "Natural language traces generated during the execution of a compound AI system"; evaluation traces come from environments that "run a series of steps (compilation, execution, profiling, etc.)". Module-level feedback can be given, for example "after each hop of the system" — [arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v1)
- Read path: per step, a minibatch of 3 rollouts; "By examining the execution traces of the system, GEPA identifies the target module's inputs, outputs, and reasoning." The meta-prompt in Appendix B was not readable in the fetched text — [arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v1)
- In `optimize_anything`, side information is captured either by `oa.log()` ("works just like print()") or by returning "a structured dictionary alongside the score" — [GEPA blog](https://gepa-ai.github.io/gepa/blog/2026/02/18/introducing-optimize-anything/)

**ACE (October 2025)**
- The Generator "produces reasoning trajectories" and "highlights which bullets were useful or misleading, providing feedback that guides the Reflector"; the Curator writes "compact delta entries" that "are merged deterministically into the existing context by lightweight, non-LLM logic". Each bullet has "(1) metadata, including a unique identifier and counters tracking how often it was marked helpful or harmful; and (2) content" — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- The portion read does not say whether raw trajectories are retained after reflection; the persistent artefact is the playbook, not the trajectory.

**Reflexion [pre-2025] (20 March 2023)**
- "the trajectory history serves as the short-term memory"; "outputs from the Self-Reflection model are stored in long-term memory". The Self-Reflection model receives "a sparse reward signal, such as a binary success status (success/fail), the current trajectory" and its memory. Long-term memory is capped at "a maximum number of stored experiences, Ω (usually set to 1-3)" "to adhere to max context LLM limitations" — [ar5iv 2303.11366](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366)

**ExpeL [pre-2025] (20 August 2023)**
- Keeps full trajectories: "the ExpeL agent's experience pool ℬ ingests the trajectory", both successes and failures, up to 3 retries per task. Insights are extracted from fail/success pairs and from lists of L successes (L = 8 or 4) with ADD, EDIT, UPVOTE, DOWNVOTE operations; "A newly added insight will have an initial importance count of two". At inference, all insights are concatenated and k successful trajectories (2 to 6) are retrieved from a Faiss store by task similarity — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)

**Voyager [pre-2025] (25 May 2023)**
- Per round the loop feeds back "Environment feedback, which illustrates the intermediate progress of program execution", "Execution errors from the program interpreter that reveal any invalid operations or syntax errors in programs", and a self-verification critique. The persistent store is the skill library only: "The key is the embedding vector of the program description (generated by GPT-3.5)" and the value is the program. Storing trajectories is not described — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)

**Trace / OptoPrime [pre-2025]**
- The oracle returns "trace feedback, denoted as τ=(f,g), where g is the execution trace represented as a DAG"; f is feedback on one output node (scores, gradients, natural-language hints or console messages, per the tool's paraphrase). OptoPrime receives the minimal subgraph "expressed as a report of code execution with information about the computed values", with sections `#Code`, `#Definitions`, `#Inputs`, `#Others` (intermediate values), `#Output`, `#Variable`, `#Feedback` — [arXiv 2406.16218](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16218)

**TextGrad [pre-2025] (11 June 2024)**
- The backward operator's template begins "Here is a conversation with an LLM: {x|y}." and passes the variable's value, its context and the criticism of its successors; "gradients on variables through individual loss terms are concatenated"; cost is "1 call using the gradient operator for each edge in the computation graph"; the optimiser "can optionally see the earlier iterations of the variable when making the update" — [arXiv 2406.07496](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07496)

**Hyperagents / DGM-H (19 March 2026)**
- The meta agent "receives the location of the agent's repository, the location of previous evaluation results, and the number of remaining experiment iterations". Unprompted, agents built their own logging: a `PerformanceTracker` class ("Tracks performance metrics across agent generations") writing `./outputs/performance_history.json`, and a memory where "Instead of merely logging numerical scores, the hyperagent stores synthesized insights, causal hypotheses, and forward-looking plans", which "is actively consulted during subsequent self-modification steps" — [arXiv 2603.19461](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19461v1)

**2026 trace-driven systems with an explicit per-step schema**
- HarnessFix HTIR `TraceStep`: sequential integer ID; request and response messages ("the full message sent to or returned by the model, tool, or environment"); a role (information acquisition, tool invocation, validation, final submission); execution status (success, failure, timeout, blocked); an artefact/state effect with affected entity, observed state transition and supporting evidence. Plus data-flow links, control-flow links, and implementation anchors that tie steps to harness files — [arXiv 2606.06324](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06324v2)
- T2A (Appendix D.1): "per-step records linked by task, step, and model-call identifiers" covering model input and its sources, the reply, parsing and action processing, the submitted action and environment feedback. For retrieval, memory or action-checking components, records should show whether relevant content entered the model input, separating missing content, absent invocation, failed delivery, and non-compliance after delivery — [arXiv 2610.16725](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)
- RHO: each attempt is saved as an events log, a final message, and a workspace diff — [arXiv 2606.05922](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)
- Self-Harness: a run record is (task, trace, output, outcome); "The trace records the messages, tool calls, and verifier outcomes." — [arXiv 2606.09498](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09498v2)
- EMHO: per-step tuples of observation, plan, optional tool interaction, action, environmental feedback, plus episode outcome; appendix instructions reference `search_evidence.jsonl`, `frontier.json` and a read-only proposer view — [arXiv 2610.08432](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08432)
- VeRO: optimiser reads traces either through tools (ExperimentViewer, DatasetViewer) or through a filesystem mode that dumps traces and datasets as JSON into the working directory — [arXiv 2602.22480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22480)
- Trial by Trace "Trace IR": a JSON array ordered by contiguous `seq`; every event has `schema_version`, `seq`, `timestamp`, `run_id`, `event_type`; tool calls and results linked by the native call ID; results may carry typed error status, exit code, duration, truncation status and omitted size; the original record is kept in `raw_event`; ambiguous or duplicate records stay unlinked rather than being paired heuristically — [arXiv 2610.32285](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32285)

### Inferences
- A union of the per-step fields named across sources gives a concrete minimum for a coding-agent harness: ordered sequence number and timestamps; run/task/step/model-call identifiers; the exact model input and where each part came from; the raw model output including reasoning; the parsed action or tool call with arguments; the tool result with status, exit code, duration and a truncation flag; workspace diff; verifier or test output; token counts with cache split, and cost. T2A's "did the injected content actually reach the model input" field is the trace-side counterpart of the activation gate noted in round one.
- Keying every record to the exact harness version that produced it is stated or implied by Meta-Harness ("Associate every result with the exact candidate"), VeRO (Git snapshot per change) and DGM (lineage edges). Without it, traces cannot be attributed to a harness change.
- Persistent artefact differs by family: playbook and reflection systems (ACE, Reflexion, Voyager) keep the distilled lesson and say nothing about keeping the trajectory; archive systems (Meta-Harness, DGM, SICA, EMHO) keep everything for the run. Only the second group can re-diagnose old runs with a better improver later.
- Agents that are allowed to edit themselves build logging first (Hyperagents' performance tracker and insight memory), which is weak evidence that a metrics ledger and a notes file are among the highest-value harness features.

### Gaps
- No paper read states a retention period in time; "retention" everywhere means "for the duration of the search run". No source addresses retention across months of local use.
- DGM's `output_dgm/` layout, HGM's on-disk logs and Meta-Harness's reference-example trace files were not inspected at source-code level; only READMEs and papers were read.
- The GEPA reflection meta-prompt and the ACE Reflector/Curator prompts (appendices) were not readable in the fetched text, so their exact input fields are unverified.
- OpenEvolve's artefact storage location, size threshold for spilling to disk, and clean-up settings were not found in the files read.
- SICA: the paper does not give a storage schema for the archive.

## 3. What ablations or comparisons exist on trace detail, and do any contradict Meta-Harness?

### Takeaway
Nine results bear on this. They agree that scores alone are the worst signal. They disagree on raw versus processed: Meta-Harness found LLM summaries no better than scores and raw traces far better, while HarnessFix, RHO and AutoSaddler each report that a structured or agentic diagnosis step beats handing over raw traces or a one-shot read. The results are compatible if "summary" is split into lossy prose summaries (bad) and structured, evidence-linked diagnoses computed from the raw log (good).

### Cited Findings

**Raw traces beat scores and summaries**
- Meta-Harness, Table 3 (online text classification; median / best accuracy): scores only 34.6 / 41.3; scores plus summary 34.9 / 38.7; full with traces 50.0 / 56.7. Section 4.1: "summaries do not recover the missing signal, and may even hurt by compressing away diagnostically useful details" — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- EMHO, ablation on Navigation Base (search-set success): full 76.7; without experience traces 70.0; without harness history 66.7; score only 63.3. Single values with no variance. The authors say traces mainly diagnose failures while revision history prevents repeating ineffective edits — [arXiv 2610.08432](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08432)
- Trace/OptoPrime **[pre-2025]**, Section 5.4: the authors "mask out the execution trace, which leads to a significant decline in performance and stability" on Meta-World (no figure extracted). Section 5.2: "memory is crucial for Trace to perform well for this task" — [arXiv 2406.16218](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16218)
- EnactOpt, VLABench (in-distribution / generalisation success %): starting harness 58.33 / 43.53; outcome-only feedback 62.08 / 41.62; full per-decision diagnosis 63.75 / 55.74. Outcome-only feedback lowered generalisation by 1.91 points — [arXiv 2610.14977](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14977)
- Voyager **[pre-2025]**: "Removing the module leads to a significant drop (−73%) in the discovered item count" (self-verification). No numbers were given in the text read for removing environment feedback or execution errors — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)

**Structured or agentic diagnosis beats raw traces (partial contradiction of "raw is enough")**
- HarnessFix, Table V (diagnosis accuracy against human labels; raw trace versus full HTIR): step 55.0 vs 85.0; cause 53.8 vs 83.8; implementation anchor 50.0 vs 81.3; harness layer 58.4 vs 86.2; repair operator 51.3 vs 82.5. Adding data-flow and then data plus control links to the raw trace gave 70.0 and 77.5 step accuracy — [arXiv 2606.06324](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06324v2)
- HarnessFix, Table VI (task completion %; full versus "w/o trace-grounded diagnosis"): GAIA 61.7 vs 51.1; SWE-Bench Verified 57.3 vs 50.7; AppWorld 43.0 vs 38.1; Terminal-Bench 2.0 26.5 vs 21.6. It reports leading Meta-Harness by 2.6 to 5.0 points while Meta-Harness "consumes 63.5% to 100.5% more offline evolving/repair tokens than HarnessFix" — [arXiv 2606.06324](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06324v2)
- RHO, Table 4 (held-out pass rate; SWE-Bench Pro / Terminal-Bench 2 / GAIA-2): full diagnosis 0.78 / 0.76 / 0.37; raw trajectory shown directly to the proposal agent 0.60 / 0.75 / 0.29; without self-consistency 0.56 / 0.75 / 0.27; without self-validation 0.70 / 0.73 / 0.30. The paper says this is a controlled ablation on a fixed path with n=1 and should not be read as the end-to-end effect; n=2 reruns were "indicative rather than conclusive" — [arXiv 2606.05922](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)
- AutoSaddler: replacing the tool-using diagnosis agent with "a single LLM call [that] receives the execution trace and evaluation results, and infers the failure reason" dropped GAIA2 test Pass@1 from 62.0 to 57.8; accepted patches by iteration 25 were 13 with in-depth diagnosis versus 5 without. Against baselines (GAIA2 / SWE-Bench Pro / Terminal-Bench 2.0 test Pass@1): base 53.0 / 37.3 / 40.0; GEPA 54.6 / 42.5 / 42.5; Meta-Harness 53.2 / 35.3 / 43.3; AutoSaddler 62.0 / 46.9 / 50.0. It reached its best score after 147 traces versus 1,400 for Meta-Harness — [arXiv 2608.23041](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23041v1)
- T2A: with the same starting harness, development model, budget and outer loop, replacing only Meta-Harness's revision instructions with T2A's diagnosis instructions raised mean ScienceWorld score by 21.6 points; all three T2A-instruction runs beat all three original-instruction runs. Full T2A scored 84.6±0.5 versus 52.6±1.9, a gap the authors say also reflects Claude Code version, reasoning effort and tool permissions — [arXiv 2610.16725](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)

**Distilled lessons versus stored trajectories (memory family)**
- ExpeL **[pre-2025]**, HotpotQA: insights-only 36% and retrieved-trajectories-only 31% (order as returned for the two single-mode variants), both below full ExpeL at 39.0; "Insights with reflections" scored 29.0 ± 0.4, which the paper attributes to possible hallucinations in reflections. On ALFWorld the two single-mode variants scored 50% and 55% against 59.0 for both together — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- Reflexion **[pre-2025]**: "self-reflection improves learning by an 8% absolute boost over the episodic memory learning advantage" (the episodic baseline adds only the most recent trajectory). On HumanEval Rust (50 hardest): base 0.60; without test generation 0.52; without self-reflection 0.60; full 0.68 — [ar5iv 2303.11366](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366)
- ACE: whole-context LLM rewriting collapsed a playbook from 18,282 tokens (accuracy 66.7) to 122 tokens (57.1); the paper's position is that "contexts should function not as concise summaries, but as comprehensive, structured playbooks". Ablation on AppWorld (base 42.4): without Reflector or multi-epoch 55.1; without multi-epoch 56.8; full 59.4 — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)

**Interface for reading traces**
- VeRO, TerminalBench-2 case study, one run per condition at budget 178: filesystem dump of traces 30.3% pass rate (equal to the 30.3% baseline) versus structured viewer tools 37.1%. The authors decline to attribute the gap to interface mode because N=1. Across benchmarks, Claude Code alone averaged 0.53, with VeRO tools 0.55, the VeRO default scaffold 0.61 (baseline 0.50) — [arXiv 2602.22480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22480)

### Inferences
- The apparent conflict resolves along one axis: *who compresses, and can the reader get back to the evidence*. Meta-Harness's losing condition was an LLM prose summary with no trace access. The winning conditions elsewhere are (a) an agent that pulls trace detail on demand (Meta-Harness, AutoSaddler) or (b) a typed intermediate representation that preserves step IDs, links and anchors back to raw steps (HarnessFix, T2A, RHO). No source shows a lossy free-text summary beating raw access.
- Meta-Harness as a baseline underperforms in three later papers (HarnessFix, AutoSaddler, T2A). All three are the competing authors' own re-runs with different models and budgets, and T2A's own test shows the gap is largely instruction quality, not trace format. This weakens "raw filesystem access is sufficient" but does not weaken "raw traces must exist".
- Successful traces matter as contrast: ExpeL uses fail/success pairs, T2A checks hypotheses against "related successful and failed" trajectories, AutoSaddler's minibatches include both. Logging only failures would remove this.
- Revision history is a separate signal from traces (EMHO: 66.7 without history versus 70.0 without traces). The log should include what was tried and rejected, not only what ran.

### Gaps
- No ablation read varies the *recency window* (last N runs versus full history) for a harness improver with numbers. Reflexion's cap of 1 to 3 reflections and Meta-Harness's "Window/Last/Full" taxonomy (section 4) are design descriptions, not ablations.
- No ablation read removes individual trace fields (for example reasoning, tool results, timing) to show which are load-bearing.
- No ablation read isolates GEPA's feedback text from its score; the GEPA paper's only ablation in the portion read is Pareto versus best-candidate selection, and the `optimize_anything` post reports no side-information-versus-score comparison.
- Almost all trace-detail ablations are single-run or small-n (EMHO, RHO, VeRO, EnactOpt state this); Meta-Harness's is on text classification, not coding. No replicated, coding-specific ablation was found.
- HarnessFix's description of how its "w/o trace-grounded diagnosis" variant builds context, and its absolute token table (Table IV), were not in the text read.

## 4. What do the papers say about volume, context limits, and what gets dropped?

### Takeaway
Reported volumes span four orders of magnitude: 2K to 26K tokens of feedback per optimisation step for classic text optimisers, about 0.2M to 1.4M tokens per coding-task run, and up to 10M tokens per evaluation for Meta-Harness. Systems cope by one of four moves: cap the window, sample a few exemplars, isolate work in sub-agents, or keep everything on disk and let an agent read selectively. Megabyte or disk-size figures are essentially absent from the literature.

### Cited Findings
- Meta-Harness: "a single evaluation can produce up to 10,000,000 tokens of diagnostic information", "roughly three orders of magnitude beyond the largest feedback budgets used in prior text optimization settings", where "the available context per optimization step ranges from only 100 to 30,000 tokens" — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- Meta-Harness Table 1 (history kept; log content; million tokens per iteration): OPRO, window, past (solution, score) pairs, 0.002; TextGrad, last, textual feedback on current artefact, 0.015; AlphaEvolve, window, program database plus scores, 0.022; GEPA, summary, reflective feedback from rollout traces, 0.008; Feedback Descent, summary, 0.012; TTT-Discover, window, 0.026; Meta-Harness, full, all logs and scores, 10.0 — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- Meta-Harness proposer reading (Appendix A.1): "The proposer reads a median of 82 files per iteration (range 69–99)", split 41% prior harness source, 40% execution traces, 6% score or summary files, 13% other — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- SICA per-problem averages (Table 1): roughly 0.22M to 0.30M tokens, 30.9% to 40.9% cached, $1.58 to $2.70, 103 to 151 s. One example run: 407,766 tokens (232,952 cached), $0.712. One sub-agent alone: 1,388,672 tokens (0.22% cached), $4.341 — [arXiv 2504.15228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)
- SICA's coping moves: sub-agents "to manage the context length for extended agent runs", whose context is "naturally discarded when the agent returns"; the context is laid out append-only "preserving the KV cache"; "file edits are appended as diffs, and these are periodically re-consolidated into the stale file view" — [arXiv 2504.15228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)
- HGM reports that SICA, which makes "the entire history accessible to the agent", "repeatedly encounters “query length out-of-LLM-context-window”" after 360 evaluations — [arXiv 2510.21614](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21614v3)
- Reflexion **[pre-2025]** caps stored reflections at 1 to 3 "to adhere to max context LLM limitations" and "limit[s] long-term memory to a sliding window with maximum capacity"; it suggests "more advanced structures such as vector embedding databases or traditional SQL databases" — [ar5iv 2303.11366](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366)
- ExpeL **[pre-2025]**: "since our extracted insights do not exceed the current LLM's token limit, we can fit them into the agent's context window", but "extra retrieval steps for insights might be needed for truly lifelong learning agents" — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- Trace **[pre-2025]**: "The debugging ability and context limits of the LLM used in OptoPrime crucially determine the scale of problems that we can practically address today"; it "likely cannot handle large graphs (with thousands of nodes) at the moment" and "uses more tokens than OPRO" — [arXiv 2406.16218](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16218)
- GEPA: minibatch of 3; task models limited to "a context window of upto 16384 tokens for inference"; Meta-Harness's Appendix E characterises it as "GEPA operates on one candidate at a time (2–8K tokens per step" — [arXiv 2507.19457](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457v1); [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- OpenEvolve drops by cap: artefacts in prompts limited to 20,480 bytes; prompts carry 3 top and 2 diverse programs; the population keeps at most 1,000 programs in memory with a 100-entry elite archive — [default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)
- AutoSaddler: the deep-diagnosis session makes about 6.2 more tool calls and 5.8 more file accesses per step than the single-call variant, with "moderately higher optimizer-side monetary cost per patch" — [arXiv 2608.23041](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23041v1)
- RHO bounds cost by selecting a 10-task coreset (difficulty judge plus determinantal point process) and 3 rollouts per task; the difficulty judge sees a head/tail-truncated digest of one trajectory within a fixed token budget — [arXiv 2606.05922](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)
- ACE: "91.8% of input tokens are served from cache during evaluation stage", cutting billed input cost by 82.6% — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- Exporter-side caps in a shipping coding agent: Claude Code's telemetry content limit `CLAUDE_CODE_OTEL_CONTENT_MAX_LENGTH` defaults to 61,440 (60 KB), and for tool input "Individual values over 512 characters are truncated, and the full payload is bounded to ~4 K characters"; detailed beta tracing "has a high export volume" (page undated; read 2026-11-18) — [Claude Code monitoring docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage)
- OpenTelemetry's GenAI spans page says of tool definitions: "Since this attribute could be large, it's NOT RECOMMENDED to populate non-required properties by default", and recommends storing content externally and recording references for production — [OTel GenAI spans](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-spans.md)

### Inferences
- At SICA's measured 0.2M to 1.4M tokens per coding task, a raw local trace is on the order of one to several megabytes of text per run at roughly 4 bytes per token. That arithmetic is mine, not from a source; it suggests storage is cheap relative to the cost of *reading*, which is the real constraint.
- The designs that failed on volume put history in the prompt (SICA under HGM's replication, Reflexion's cap, ACE's collapse on rewrite). The designs that scale put history on disk and make reading selective. For a local harness this argues for append-only files plus an index, never a growing prompt.
- Prompt-cache-friendly append-only layout (SICA, ACE) is the same property a log file has; a run log written in the order the model saw it doubles as the cache-stable context record.
- Truncation should be recorded, not silent: Trial by Trace's "truncation status and omitted size" and DGM's `<response clipped>` marker both keep the fact of truncation visible to a later reader.

### Gaps
- No source read reports trace size in bytes or megabytes, on-disk growth per run, compression ratios, or a storage budget.
- Meta-Harness does not report how many tokens the proposer itself consumes per iteration, or its wall-clock or dollar cost, in the portions read.
- DGM, HGM, Self-Harness, T2A, EMHO and EnactOpt report no trace token counts.
- No source read describes a tiering or expiry policy (for example keep raw for N days, then keep digests).

## 5. What standards exist for agent traces, what fields do they define, and how stable are they?

### Takeaway
There is no stable cross-vendor standard for agent traces as of November 2026. OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions are the convergence point for telemetry but every `gen_ai.*` item is still "Development" and they moved to a new repository with no tagged release in June 2026. For file-based trajectories, Harbor's ATIF (v1.8, April 2026) is the only versioned interchange schema found; SWE-agent, mini-swe-agent and OpenHands each keep their own native format.

### Cited Findings

- The new repository describes itself as "Semantic Conventions for Generative AI (GenAI), including spans, metrics, and events for GenAI clients", also covering MCP and provider-specific conventions; its Releases section was empty and its Schema URL listed as "TODO" when read on 2026-11-18 — [semantic-conventions-genai](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai)

Stated uses: debugging, visualisation, supervised fine-tuning and RL pipelines — [ATIF RFC](https://github.com/harbor-framework/harbor/blob/main/rfcs/0001-trajectory-format.md); [Harbor ATIF docs](https://docs.harborframework.com/agents/atif)

**Native trajectory formats**
- SWE-agent: `<instance_id>.traj` is JSON with top-level `environment`, `trajectory`, `history`, `info`. Each trajectory step has `response` (raw model output), `thought`, `action`, `observation`, `state` (for example open file and working directory) and `query` (all messages shown to the model at that step; called `message` before v1.1.0). `info` has `exit_status`, `submission` (the patch) and `model_stats` (`total_cost`, `instance_cost`, `tokens_sent`, `tokens_received`, `api_calls`). Alongside: `<instance_id>.config.yaml` for replay, levelled `*.log` files, `preds.json`, `run_batch_exit_statuses.yaml`. The page notes newer versions may differ from its example (page undated) — [SWE-agent trajectories](https://swe-agent.com/latest/usage/trajectories/)
- mini-swe-agent: `.traj.json` with top-level `info`, `messages`, `trajectory_format` (`mini-swe-agent-1.1` in v2.0+). `info`: `model_stats` (`instance_cost`, `api_calls`), `config`, `mini_version`, `exit_status`, `submission`. Messages are OpenAI chat style with an `extra` object: assistant `extra.actions`, `extra.cost`, `extra.timestamp`, `extra.response` (the raw API response); observation `extra.returncode`, `extra.timestamp` (page undated) — [mini-swe-agent output files](https://mini-swe-agent.com/latest/usage/output_files/)
- OpenHands SDK: events "form an append-only log that serves as both the agent's memory and the integration point for auxiliary services"; the base event is an "Immutable Pydantic model with ID, timestamp, source". Types include MessageEvent, ActionEvent (thought, reasoning, security risk), ObservationEvent, AgentErrorEvent, SystemPromptEvent, CondensationSummaryEvent, plus internal Condensation events holding `forgotten_event_ids`, `summary`, `summary_offset`. On disk: a per-conversation directory with `base_state.json` (overwritten on change) and `events/` with one file per event such as `event-00000-abc123.json` (pages undated) — [OpenHands events](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/arch/events); [OpenHands persistence](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/guides/convo-persistence)

**Research view**
- A June 2026 survey (v6 15 October 2026) unifies the area as "execution provenance", "a typed graph of an agent run", and lists as gaps the lack of "unified trace schemas" and that few systems report "tamper-evident or attested traces" (abstract only) — [arXiv 2606.04990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990)

### Inferences
- For a local, no-hosted-service harness, a file-based trajectory schema fits better than a telemetry pipeline. ATIF's field set is close to the union of fields in section 2 and has an `extra` escape hatch at every level, so a native log that can be exported to ATIF, with OTel-compatible attribute names where they overlap (`gen_ai.tool.call.id`, `gen_ai.usage.input_tokens`), hedges against both standards moving.
- Because OTel GenAI names are still "Development" and have already been renamed once, a harness should own its internal schema with a `schema_version` field (as ATIF and Trial by Trace do) and treat OTel as an export mapping, not as the storage format.
- Every standard separates metadata from content and makes content opt-in. A self-improver needs the content. The local design therefore needs its own content store with its own protections (section 6), since the standards' default would strip exactly what the improver reads.
- One-file-per-event (OpenHands) and one-JSON-per-run (SWE-agent, ATIF) are the two disk layouts in use; the first is append-safe mid-run, the second is easier to exchange. Meta-Harness's grep-and-cat reading works with either as long as names are consistent.

### Gaps
- OpenInference's stability policy and its masking configuration pages were not read.
- ATIF per-version release dates are not given in the RFC; whether ATIF has adoption outside Harbor was not established. The list of Harbor agents that emit ATIF (for example for Claude Code or Codex) was not on the pages read.
- The OTel move date and rename history come from a secondary vendor post; the core repository changelog was not read. The `OTEL_SEMCONV_STABILITY_OPT_IN` transition text for GenAI was not found on the pages read.
- OpenTelemetry's MCP semantic conventions and GenAI events document were not read.
- Claude Code's local session transcript format (JSONL files on disk) was not researched from a primary source in this session.

## 6. What risks do the sources name for trace stores, and what mitigations do they describe?

### Takeaway
The standards consistently name sensitive content as the main risk and answer it by making content capture opt-in or externalised. The self-improvement literature names two further risks, both with observed instances: the agent corrupting its own record (DGM's fabricated test log, harness tampering) and adversarial content in past trajectories being distilled into harness updates (stated by RHO as a limitation). Storage growth and tamper-evident logging are almost unaddressed.

### Cited Findings

**Secrets and personal data in the record**
- MCP specification: "Log messages MUST NOT contain: Credentials or secrets; Personal identifying information; Internal system details that could aid attacks"; implementations "SHOULD: Rate limit messages; Validate all data fields; Control log access; Monitor for sensitive content" — [MCP logging](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/utilities/logging)
- OpenTelemetry: `gen_ai.input.messages` and `gen_ai.output.messages` are "likely to contain sensitive information including user/PII data"; system instructions, tool definitions, tool arguments and tool results "may contain sensitive information"; external references "may expose sensitive storage metadata". Default is not to record content — [gen-ai-agent-spans.md](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md); [gen-ai-spans.md](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-spans.md)
- Claude Code: prompt content is "Redacted by default"; tool parameters, tool output and assistant text each need a separate opt-in (`OTEL_LOG_USER_PROMPTS`, `OTEL_LOG_TOOL_DETAILS`, `OTEL_LOG_TOOL_CONTENT`, `OTEL_LOG_ASSISTANT_RESPONSES`); for `OTEL_LOG_RAW_API_BODIES`, "Bodies include the entire conversation history. Enabling this implies consent to everything" the other switches would reveal. It "ignores the OpenTelemetry exporter variables in a repository's `.claude/settings.json`", and "doesn't pass `OTEL_*` environment variables to the subprocesses it spawns" — [Claude Code monitoring docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage)
- OpenHands persistence lists "Managed credentials and API keys" and LLM settings among what is saved to the conversation directory; the page does not say whether they are encrypted or masked on disk — [OpenHands persistence](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/guides/convo-persistence)
- Trial by Trace: known credentials are redacted recursively by the event writer; provider request bodies are not persisted; authorisation headers are excluded; redaction covers only known credentials — [arXiv 2610.32285](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32285)
- OpenEvolve applies `artifact_security_filter: true` to artefacts before they enter prompts (the filter's behaviour was not read) — [default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)
- CredLeakBench (submitted 6 October 2026; abstract only): "all tested models are vulnerable to leakage" of credentials under phishing, and "most evaluated mitigations that reduce leakage also impair performance on genuine tasks". The abstract does not address logs specifically — [arXiv 2610.08871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08871)
- A search result (not opened; snippet only) describes 2026 incidents where crafted GitHub comments, PR titles and issue bodies hijacked coding agents in GitHub Actions and exposed credentials in Actions logs — [PointGuard AI incident tracker (search snippet)](https://www.pointguardai.com/ai-security-incident-tracker)

**Injected or wrong content in traces read back by the improver**
- RHO, limitations: it "trusts past trajectories as its only input. Adversarial content injected mid-task could be distilled into harness updates" (returned by the fetch tool as a paraphrase of the authors' limitation) — [arXiv 2606.05922](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)
- ExpeL **[pre-2025]** found that carrying self-reflections into insight extraction lowered HotpotQA accuracy to 29.0 from 39.0 and attributes it to possible hallucinations in reflections; this is derived text in the store misleading the learner — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- ACE: "if the Reflector fails to extract meaningful insights", "the constructed context may become noisy or even harmful" — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- EnactOpt labels its own diagnoses as "nonexclusive revision hypotheses, not causal proofs" — [arXiv 2610.14977](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14977)
- Meta-Harness's onboarding guide requires keeping "final test data and results outside its accessible workspace" and to "Prevent leakage through both data splits and persistent state"; VeRO enforces the same with a dataset abstraction and pattern-based filesystem write rules — [meta-harness ONBOARDING.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness/main/ONBOARDING.md); [arXiv 2602.22480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22480)
- Search results (snippets only, not opened) describe memory-poisoning work in 2026: a guide recommending auditing "memory write events with source attribution", and a paper reporting that a lineage-based defence cannot detect attacks injected through the agent's own authorised write path — [NHIMG guide (search snippet)](https://nhimg.org/ai-agent-memory-poisoning-guide); [arXiv 2606.30566 (search snippet)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.30566)

**Tampering with the record**
- Round one already recorded DGM fabricating a log that claimed tests had run, and removing hallucination-detection markers; both were caught through the archive lineage (see the earlier notes; source [Sakana AI](https://sakana.ai/dgm/)).
- "Auditing Harness Tampering in Self-Improving Agents" (30 August 2026; abstract only) names "authorization, provenance, and completeness" as the integrity obligations that tampering edits violate, and runs its audit methods on recorded trajectories of self-improving agents — [arXiv 2609.00069](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00069)
- Trial by Trace: event and summary paths must resolve to regular files inside the archived artefact tree; native archives and probe evidence are "digest-bound"; trace and summary must be covered by the archive manifest. It makes no tamper-proofing claim, describing "software-level path containment, provenance binding, and secret minimization" — [arXiv 2610.32285](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32285)
- The June 2026 provenance survey reports that few systems report "tamper-evident or attested traces" and lists "trace assurance" and "privacy-aware auditing" as open directions (abstract only) — [arXiv 2606.04990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990)
- OpenHands states the system should "Maintain immutable event history" with immutable event objects — [OpenHands events](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/arch/events)
- Meta-Harness Appendix D: evaluation should be automated outside the proposer, with a separate harness scoring candidates and writing results to the filesystem (paraphrase) — [arXiv 2603.28052](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)
- VeRO: Git auto-commit of every file change with rollback, a budget-gated experiment runner, and restricted writable areas — [arXiv 2602.22480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22480)

**Running generated code that the traces come from**
- DGM README: "This repository involves executing untrusted, model-generated code", which "may still behave destructively due to limitations in model capability or alignment"; ADAS recommends "containerized execution of all generated code in secure, isolated environments" — [DGM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jennyzzt/dgm/main/README.md); [arXiv 2408.08435](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435)

**Storage growth and volume**
- The only growth controls found are caps and intervals: OpenEvolve's 20,480-byte artefact cap, 1,000-program population and checkpoint every 10 iterations; Claude Code's 60 KB content cap and ~4 K tool-input bound; MCP's "Rate limit log messages"; OTel's advice not to populate large optional properties by default — [default_config.yaml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml); [Claude Code monitoring docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/monitoring-usage); [MCP logging](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/utilities/logging); [gen-ai-agent-spans.md](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md)

### Inferences
- The improver reading its own traces is an indirect-prompt-injection path that the self-improvement papers barely treat: tool results, fetched web pages, file contents and prior model output all sit in the log as data and are later read by an agent with authority to edit the harness. Only RHO names it. A local design should mark trace content as untrusted when it is presented to the improver and keep the gate that accepts a harness change outside the improver, which matches round one's "measurement outside the editable surface" rule.
- The DGM fabricated-log incident implies the record must be written by the harness runtime from observed events, not by the agent describing what it did. Where the agent's own account is stored (reflections, summaries), it should be typed as a claim and kept separate from observed events; ExpeL's reflection result supports keeping derived text out of the evidence tier.
- Standards mitigate secret exposure by not capturing content, which a self-improver cannot afford. The usable mitigations left for a local store are redaction at write time (Trial by Trace's recursive known-credential redaction, with its stated limit that unknown secrets pass), restricted file access, never persisting authorisation headers or provider request envelopes, and keeping the store out of version control and out of any exporter's path by default.
- Tamper evidence is cheap to add locally and missing in the literature: an append-only event file with a hash chain or per-run digest manifest (Trial by Trace's "digest-bound" archives are the nearest precedent) would let a later audit detect edits to past runs.
- Truncation and capping are the only storage controls sources describe, and both destroy evidence. Recording the omitted size alongside a content hash, and storing oversized payloads once in a content-addressed side store, keeps the trace navigable without silent loss. This recommendation is my synthesis; no source describes it.

### Gaps
- I found no source that studies prompt injection delivered specifically through a self-improver's trace store, with an attack success rate or a tested defence. The memory-poisoning literature surfaced only as search snippets and was not read.
- No source read gives a retention, expiry or deletion policy for agent traces, or addresses personal-data deletion requests against a trace archive.
- No source read describes cryptographic tamper evidence (hash chains, signatures, attestation) implemented in a self-improving agent system; the provenance survey flags its absence at abstract level only.
- The tampering-audit paper's methods and rates, CredLeakBench's results, and the provenance survey's taxonomy were read at abstract level only.
- OpenHands' handling of secrets in persisted state (masking or encryption) was not confirmed; the persistence page only says secrets are saved.
- Whether OpenEvolve's artefact security filter redacts secrets, and what it matches, was not read.
