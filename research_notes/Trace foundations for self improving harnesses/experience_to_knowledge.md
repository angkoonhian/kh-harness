# From recorded experience to reusable knowledge: architectures, evidence and upkeep (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-18. This file extends `Self improving agents and harnesses/agent_self_improvement_science.md` and `failure_modes_and_controls.md` and does not repeat their content; where a round-1 statement is corrected or confirmed here, the bullet says so.

Reading-depth labels used on every finding:

- **[BODY]** the number or claim was read from the paper body, appendix or the primary page during this session.
- **[ABSTRACT]** only the abstract page was read.
- **[SNIPPET]** seen only in a search-result summary; not opened.
- **[SECONDARY]** read from a third-party summary or practitioner page, not the primary source.

Papers were read through a page-to-text fetch tool that returns an extraction of the page, not the raw page; table values are as that extraction reported them. Where a paper's prose and tables disagree, the disagreement is flagged.

## 1. What are the main designs, and what does each store, when does it write, how does it retrieve, and what effect was measured?

### Takeaway
The designs differ on four axes: the unit stored (raw trajectory, abstracted workflow, one-line insight, titled strategy item, executable function, file or wiki page), the admission test (none, an LLM judge, an executed test, a human), the delivery path (everything in the prompt, top-k embedding retrieval, model-chosen file reads, a callable function) and upkeep (none, counters, LLM merge, offline consolidation). The largest and most repeatable gains come from designs whose unit is abstracted away from the instance, admitted after some check, and delivered in small quantity (ReasoningBank's best setting is one item; SkillsBench's best is one to three skills).

**Generative Agents-style reflection [background, pre-2025]** (Park et al.; arXiv v2 6 August 2023; UIST '23)
- [BODY] The memory stream holds natural-language observations with creation and last-access timestamps. Retrieval scores each memory by recency (exponential decay, factor 0.995 per game hour), importance (LLM rating 1 to 10 at write time) and relevance (embedding cosine), min-max normalised and summed with all weights 1 — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- [BODY] Reflection fires when the summed importance of recent events exceeds 150 (about two to three times per simulated day). The model reads the 100 most recent memories, proposes three questions, retrieves on them, and writes up to five insights, each citing the memory numbers that support it; reflections can cite reflections, forming a tree — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- [BODY] Ablation (believability TrueSkill μ): full 29.89; no reflection 26.88; no reflection or planning 25.64; no observation, reflection or planning 21.21; 100 participants. Reported failure modes are retrieval misses and embellishment; 6 of 453 responses (1.3%) hallucinated a relationship — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)

**Voyager [background, pre-2025]** (Wang et al.; arXiv v2 19 October 2023)
- [BODY] A skill is executable code. It enters the library only after a GPT-4 critic confirms the task succeeded; failed tasks are logged instead, with at most four code-generation rounds per task. The key is the embedding of a GPT-3.5-written description; retrieval returns the top-5 skills for the current plan plus environment feedback — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- [BODY] Ablations on discovered item count: random instead of automatic curriculum −93%; no self-verification −73%; without the skill library the agent "exhibits a tendency to plateau in the later stages" (no percentage given). With Voyager's skill library, AutoGPT solved three of four unseen tasks in at least one of three trials (0/3 on all four without it) — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)

**Synapse [background, pre-2025]** (Zheng et al.; v1 13 June 2023; ICLR 2024)
- [ABSTRACT] Stores full trajectories of abstracted states and actions as exemplars and retrieves them by embedding similarity. Reports 99.2% average success on 64 MiniWoB++ tasks (a 10% relative gain) and a 56% relative gain in step success rate on Mind2Web over the previous prompting state of the art — [arXiv 2306.07863](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.07863)
- [BODY, via later papers] As a baseline in ReasoningBank's tables Synapse adds +1.0 to +1.6 points on WebArena over no memory, and is slightly below no memory on SWE-bench Verified with Gemini 2.5 Pro (53.4 vs 54.0) — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)

**ExpeL [background, pre-2025]** (Zhao et al.; 20 August 2023; AAAI-24)
- [BODY] Two stores: a pool of trajectories gathered with Reflexion-style retries, from which top-k *successful* trajectories are retrieved as few-shot examples (Faiss kNN on task similarity; k = 6 HotpotQA, 2 ALFWorld and WebShop), and an insight list that is placed in the prompt in full — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- [BODY] Insights are extracted by comparing a failed and a successful attempt at the same task, and from chunks of successes. The extractor edits the list with ADD (importance starts at 2), EDIT, UPVOTE (increment) and DOWNVOTE (decrement); an insight is removed when its count reaches zero — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- [BODY] Results with gpt-3.5-turbo: HotpotQA 28.0 → 39.0; ALFWorld 40.0 → 59.0 (vs ReAct). Ablation, insights only / retrieval only: HotpotQA 36 / 31; ALFWorld 50 / 55; WebShop 37 / 38. Insights extracted by gpt-3.5 instead of gpt-4 scored 32.0 on HotpotQA, the same as hand-crafted insights (32.0), versus 39.0 for gpt-4 insights. Feeding Reflexion's reflections into extraction hurt (29.0 vs 39.0). Transfer of HotpotQA insights to FEVER: 70 vs 63 for ReAct — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- Inconsistency flag: the prose says ExpeL outperforms Reflexion on ALFWorld but quotes figures that do not support the statement; Table 2 (ExpeL retrieve-only 54.5 at round 0 vs ReAct+Reflexion 54.4 at round 3) is the reliable source — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)

**MemGPT and Letta, sleep-time compute and "dreaming"**
- [ABSTRACT] MemGPT (Packer et al.; 12 October 2023) proposes "virtual context management" over memory tiers, evaluated on document analysis and multi-session chat. No task-success number for experience reuse was read — [arXiv 2310.08560](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560)
- [ABSTRACT] Sleep-time compute (Lin, Snell, Wang, Packer, Wooders, Stoica, Gonzalez; 17 April 2025): the model reasons offline about a context before any query arrives. About 5x less test-time compute for equal accuracy on Stateful GSM-Symbolic and Stateful AIME; scaling sleep-time compute raises accuracy by up to 13% and 18% respectively; amortising across related queries cuts average cost per query 2.5x; effectiveness correlates with how predictable the query is from the context — [arXiv 2504.13171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13171)
- [BODY, product documentation, undated] Letta's current docs describe a git-backed memory filesystem (MemFS) that the agent can inspect and edit, and "Dreaming": background subagents review recent conversations, consolidate lessons and update memory, triggered "after a set number of completed agent steps or when the context window is compacted". An optional setting has a second background conversation review proposed updates before they are applied; it does not ask a human. `/doctor` audits memory placement, duplication and token usage. The page reports no measurement — [Letta docs](https://docs.letta.com/configuration/memory)

**Agent Workflow Memory [background, pre-2025]** (Wang, Mao, Fried, Neubig; 11 September 2024) — extends round 1
- [BODY] A workflow is a goal description plus steps, each with an environment-state description, the reasoning and an executable action; example-specific values are replaced by variables (for example "{product-name}"). The induction prompt asks for sub-routines that recur across tasks, at least two steps each — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)
- [BODY] Online mode: after each test task an LLM evaluator labels the trajectory; only trajectories judged successful become workflows. The labels are not ground truth. Memory is kept per website — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)
- [BODY] WebArena (GPT-4): 35.5% success at 5.9 steps, versus 23.5% for BrowserGym as reported and 33.0% for SteP, which uses 14 human-written workflows. Mind2Web offline cross-task step success 45.1 vs 36.2 (MindAct) — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)
- [BODY] Ablations: rule-based induction (deduplicate by action sequence, drop invalid steps) matched LM induction on WebArena (35.6 vs 35.5) but not on Mind2Web (step SR 43.4 vs 45.1); text versus code workflows made no substantial difference (45.4 vs 45.1); exposing workflows as callable actions was used in only 18.5% of tasks — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)
- [BODY] Stated limits: online induction can learn from wrongly-judged trajectories; combining offline and online workflows was worse than offline alone (task SR 1.6 vs 4.8); workflows can push the agent to follow a routine where it does not fit, lowering action F1 (57.3 vs 60.6) — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)
- Inconsistency flag: the abstract's "8.9 to 14.0" point range for online gains does not match the body's per-split figures (round 1 quoted the abstract) — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)

**A-MEM and Mem0 (conversational memory, not task learning)**
- [BODY] A-MEM (Xu et al.; arXiv 2502.12110, v11 8 October 2025): each note has content, timestamp, LLM-generated keywords, tags, a contextual description, an embedding and links. On write, an LLM chooses links among the nearest notes and may rewrite the neighbours' descriptions and tags ("memory evolution"). LoCoMo with GPT-4o-mini: multi-hop F1 45.85 vs 25.52 for MemGPT, but single-hop 50.03 vs 69.23 for the full-context LoCoMo baseline; about 2,520 tokens per question vs about 16,900 — [arXiv 2502.12110](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110)
- [BODY] Mem0 (Chhikara et al.; 28 April 2025): extracts facts from each message pair; for each candidate an LLM picks ADD, UPDATE, DELETE (contradicted memory is removed) or NOOP against the top-10 similar memories. The graph variant marks contradicted relations invalid instead of deleting them. On LOCOMO (LLM-judge score) Mem0 66.88 and Mem0g 68.44, against 72.90 for simply giving the model the full conversation (about 26,000 tokens); Mem0 uses 1,764 tokens and has p95 latency 1.44 s vs 17.1 s — [arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)
- [BODY] The two papers disagree about A-MEM: Mem0's table gives A-MEM an overall judge score of 48.38, the lowest of the memory systems it compares — [arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413); contradicted in direction by A-MEM's own F1 results in [arXiv 2502.12110](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12110)

**Dynamic Cheatsheet** (Suzgun et al.; 10 April 2025) — extends round 1
- [BODY] Two variants. DC-Cu: answer first, then the same model, prompted as curator, rewrites the cheatsheet. DC-RS: retrieve the top-3 similar past input-output pairs, curate, then answer. The curator has no labels and judges correctness itself — [arXiv 2504.07952](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07952)
- [BODY] Claude 3.5 Sonnet: AIME 2024 23.3 → 50.0 (DC-Cu); AIME 2025 6.7 → 36.7; Math Equation Balancer 44.8 → 100. GPT-4o Game of 24: 10.0 → 99.0 (DC-RS), driven by storing and reusing a Python solver — [arXiv 2504.07952](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07952)
- [BODY] Failure cases: GPT-4o-mini AIME 2024 fell from 16.7 to 13.3; GPT-4o MMLU-Pro Engineering fell from 53.2 to 44.0 (DC-Cu); reasoning models (o1, DeepSeek R1) showed minimal or inconsistent gains; the curator sometimes abbreviated prior memory to a placeholder ("Previous content […] preserved"), losing stored content; the authors state that faulty heuristics in memory can be amplified — [arXiv 2504.07952](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07952)

**SkillWeaver** (arXiv 2504.07079; April 2025)
- [BODY] A skill is a Python function wrapping Playwright code, with a docstring stating the prerequisite page state and a usage log. Pipeline: the agent proposes practice tasks, attempts them, an LLM reward model judges success, successful trajectories are converted to a function, static checks reject known mistakes, and a "honing" stage runs the function as a test (with LLM-generated arguments when parameters exist) and debugs failures. 160 exploration iterations per website — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)
- [BODY] WebArena success: GPT-4o 22.6 → 29.8; GPT-4o-mini 9.2 → 14.1 using functions synthesised by GPT-4o. Live sites: 40.2 → 56.2 average, with no change on one of four sites (11.1 → 11.1). Failure cases are choosing the wrong function and passing wrong arguments — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)
- Inconsistency flag: the abstract's "up to 54.3%" gain for weaker agents does not appear in the tables — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)

**Memp, procedural memory** (arXiv 2508.06433; August 2025)
- [BODY] Compares three stored forms: the verbatim trajectory, a script-like abstraction, and both ("proceduralization"). ALFWorld test success with GPT-4o: no memory 42.14; script 56.43; trajectory 74.29; both 77.86. On TravelPlanner the script form *lowered* hard-constraint pass rate for GPT-4o (12.88 → 5.50) and all memory forms were below no memory on that metric — [arXiv 2508.06433](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06433)
- [BODY] Update strategies compared: append everything, keep only successful trajectories, and revise a memory after a retrieval that led to failure; the text says the reflection-based revision was best, by +0.7 points and 14 fewer steps over the second-best, with per-strategy values only in a figure. GPT-4o's memory given to Qwen2.5-14B raised TravelPlanner completion by 5% and cut 1.6 steps. Retrieving too many memories hurts. The authors state the method relies on benchmark-supplied reward signals — [arXiv 2508.06433](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06433)

**ReasoningBank** (Google and UIUC; 29 September 2025; ICLR 2026) — replaces round 1's snippet-level entry
- [BODY] Unit: a memory item with a title, a one-sentence description and content (distilled reasoning steps or operational insight); at most three items per trajectory. Successful trajectories yield validated strategies; failed ones yield pitfalls and counterfactual lessons. Success or failure is decided by an LLM judge with the same backbone and no ground truth; its accuracy against ground truth on WebArena-Shopping was 72.7% — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)
- [BODY] Retrieval: embed the task query, cosine top-k, default k = 1. Consolidation: "newly generated items are directly added without additional pruning"; merging and forgetting are left to future work — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)
- [BODY] WebArena overall success (no memory / Synapse / AWM / ReasoningBank): Gemini 2.5 Flash 40.5 / 42.1 / 44.1 / 48.8; Gemini 2.5 Pro 46.7 / 47.7 / 47.6 / 53.9; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 41.7 / 42.6 / 40.8 / 46.3. SWE-bench Verified resolve rate (no memory / Synapse / ReasoningBank): Flash 34.2 / 35.4 / 38.8; Pro 54.0 / 53.4 / 57.4, with 2.8 and 1.3 fewer steps. Mind2Web cross-task task SR, Flash: 3.3 → 4.8 — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)
- [BODY] The paper reports no experiment that varies judge accuracy, although it asserts robustness to judge noise — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)

**ACE playbooks** (arXiv 2510.04618 v3; ICLR 2026) — extends round 1
- [BODY] A bullet has metadata (unique identifier, counters of how often it was marked helpful or harmful) and content. The Generator flags which bullets were useful or misleading on the current task, the Reflector proposes lessons, the Curator writes only the new insight, and deterministic code merges it — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- [BODY] Appendix sensitivity: embedding-dedup thresholds of 50%, 70%, 90% gave 77.0, 73.9, 78.6 on FiNER (base 70.7); maximum context of 10K, 50K, 100K tokens gave 78.6, 78.4, 78.3. Component ablation on AppWorld: without Reflector and multi-epoch 55.1; without multi-epoch 56.8; full 59.4 — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- [BODY] The pages read do not state that the counters drive pruning or retrieval. Contradiction detection, prioritising high-confidence updates and periodic pruning are named as possible extensions, not as implemented features — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)

**Anthropic Agent Skills** (engineering post, 16 October 2025)
- [BODY] A skill is a folder with a `SKILL.md` (YAML frontmatter `name` and `description`) plus optional scripts and resources. Progressive disclosure: only name and description are loaded at start; the body is read when the skill looks relevant; linked files are read on demand; bundled scripts can run without their text entering context — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills)
- [BODY] Authoring guidance: start from evaluation (find where the agent struggles on representative tasks, then write the skill for that gap); iterate with Claude to capture successful approaches and common mistakes. Agents creating, editing and evaluating their own skills is stated as a future hope, not a shipped capability — [Anthropic engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills)

**Anthropic memory tool and "The components of agent memory"**
- [BODY, product documentation] The memory tool (`memory_20250818`) is a client-side file store under `/memories` with `view`, `create`, `str_replace`, `insert`, `delete`, `rename`. The model decides what to write. The API injects an instruction to view the memory directory before doing anything else and to record progress as it goes. The documentation's upkeep advice is to cap file sizes, "periodically delete memory files that haven't been accessed in a long time", and validate paths; it reports no measurements — [Claude platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool)
- [BODY] "The components of agent memory" (Anthropic engineering, Lance Martin, 6 November 2026) describes a hierarchy: searchable transcripts, memory notes the model writes, memory representations such as wikis, and a small injected layer loaded every session (Claude Code loads the first 200 lines or 25 KB of `MEMORY.md`). "Dreaming" is an offline pass in which the model checks notes against transcripts, merges duplicates, prunes, repairs links, builds wiki pages and promotes items into the injected layer — [Anthropic engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)
- [BODY] Measured in that post: CL-Bench database task mean reward, stateless → with memory: Opus 5.5 0.655 → 0.838; Sonnet 5.5 0.644 → 0.833; Opus 5 0.564 → 0.828; Opus 4.7 0.383 → 0.700; Sonnet 4.6 0.330 → 0.364, rising to 0.641 with refined prompting. On a 25-question, roughly 420M-token corpus, a wiki built over 128 dreaming sessions answered 13% → 48% alone and 61% with search, vs 53% for search alone — [Anthropic engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)
- [BODY] Negative results in the same post: instructing Opus 5 and Opus 4.7 to "record only failures" cut their memory gains by 0.234 and 0.150; an incorrect note made the agent loop and abandon a correct route, and such a note "can be carried into every session"; an older model wrote 413 files in 225 folders, spent turns combing them and earned one badge where a newer model wrote about ten files and earned four; one wiki grew to 1.77 times its source corpus; concurrent writes to a shared file erased facts — [Anthropic engineering](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)

**2026 work on memory for coding agents**
- [BODY] "Structurally Aligned Subtask-Level Memory for Software Engineering Agents" (Shen et al., Kuaishou; 25 February 2026). Unit: (category in {Analyze, Reproduce, Edit, Verify}, subtask description, abstracted experience with file paths and other repository-specific detail removed). Written after each subtask; the extractor first judges correctness, then extracts a success pattern or a failure-avoidance strategy. Retrieval: hard filter to the current category, then top-1 by embedding — [arXiv 2602.21611](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21611)
- [BODY] SWE-bench Verified, Mini SWE Agent scaffold, 500 instances, memory starting empty, three shuffled runs; Pass@1 vanilla / instance-level (ReasoningBank) / subtask-level: Gemini 2.5 Flash 36.3 / 37.8 / 41.9; Gemini 2.5 Pro 53.5 / 55.1 / 60.3; Claude 3.7 Sonnet 52.2 / 51.1 / 56.1; Claude 4.0 Sonnet 63.5 / 63.3 / 65.8 — [arXiv 2602.21611](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21611)
- [BODY] Usage analysis: of 797 distinct retrieved entries, 50.7% were used once; the top 100 accounted for 33.8% of retrievals. Net resolved-instance change by stream position: −1 for instances 1 to 200, +3 for 201 to 300, +9 and +10 for 301 to 500. Gains concentrate on hard tasks (35.5% → 44.2%) versus +1.8 points on easy ones — [arXiv 2602.21611](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21611)
- [ABSTRACT] MemGovern (Wang et al.; 11 January 2026) converts GitHub issue-tracker history into 135K "experience cards" searched by the agent, and reports a 4.65% improvement in SWE-bench Verified resolution rate; card schema and baselines were not read — [arXiv 2601.06789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06789)
- [SNIPPET] MemCoder (arXiv 2603.13258, March 2026) stores human-verified intent-to-code mappings and reports a 9.4% resolved-rate improvement; SWE-MeM (arXiv 2606.28434, June 2026) trains agents to manage in-trajectory memory and is about context compression, not cross-task reuse — [arXiv 2603.13258](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.13258v1); [arXiv 2606.28434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.28434)

### Inferences
- Three delivery models exist and they fail differently. "Everything in the prompt" (ExpeL insights, DC-Cu, ACE playbook, AGENTS.md) pays a token and compliance cost on every task. "Embedding top-k" (ReasoningBank, Synapse, Memp) fails by retrieving something similar-looking but wrong. "Model chooses to open it" (skills, memory files) fails by never being opened. The SkillsBench audit below found exactly the third failure.
- Only four designs test a unit before admitting it with something other than an LLM's opinion of the trajectory: Voyager (critic on environment state), SkillWeaver (static checks plus executed tests), ExpeL's offline setting (benchmark outcome) and human-authored skills. These are also the designs whose unit is executable or human-reviewed.
- Conversational-memory systems (Mem0, A-MEM, Letta blocks) answer "what did the user say"; their benchmark is LOCOMO question answering, where the full-context baseline still wins on accuracy (72.90 vs 66.88 to 68.44). They are evidence about cost and latency, not about an agent getting better at a task.
- The closest published analogue to a coding-harness learning loop is the subtask-level memory paper: it is the only one read that measures a streaming, from-empty memory on SWE-bench Verified with variance, and its gains (+2.3 to +6.8 points) shrink as the base model gets stronger.

### Gaps
- MemGPT, Synapse, sleep-time compute and MemGovern were read at abstract level only; MemCoder and SWE-MeM only as search snippets.
- I found no official Anthropic documentation page for Claude Code's "auto dream" consolidation; descriptions of its triggers (for example 24 hours and 5 sessions) come from third-party blogs and are not cited as fact here.
- Letta's documentation page gives no numeric default for the dreaming trigger and no evaluation.
- No head-to-head of ACE-style playbooks against ReasoningBank-style retrieval on a coding benchmark was found.
- LangChain's memory products (LangMem) appear only as a Mem0 baseline; no LangChain primary source was read.

## 2. What does the evidence say about failures versus successes, raw trajectories versus distilled insight, and the granularity of the stored unit?

### Takeaway
Failures add value only when they are converted into an abstract lesson and mixed with successes; failure-only recording and failure trajectories added to exemplar or workflow stores did not help or hurt. Distilled units beat raw trajectories when the next task differs from the stored one, raw trajectories win when it is near-identical, and the measured sweet spot for granularity is a small, categorised, single-purpose unit retrieved one at a time.

### Cited Findings

**Failures versus successes**
- [BODY] ReasoningBank, WebArena-Shopping, Gemini 2.5 Flash, success-only → success plus failure: ReasoningBank 46.5 → 49.7; Synapse 40.6 → 41.7; AWM 44.4 → 42.2. - [BODY] ExpeL extracts insights by contrasting a failed and a successful attempt at the same task, but retrieves only successful trajectories as examples — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)

- [BODY] ReasoningBank, number of retrieved items k (WebArena-Shopping, Flash): k=0 39.0; k=1 49.7; k=2 46.0; k=3 45.5; k=4 44.4. Authors: "excessive experiences may introduce conflicts or noise" — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)

- [BODY] Instance-level memory (ReasoningBank) on SWE-bench Verified was below vanilla for Claude 3.7 Sonnet (51.1 vs 52.2) and flat for Claude 4.0 Sonnet (63.3 vs 63.5); subtask-level memory gained on both. Retrieval without the category filter gave 53.8 against 56.1 with it — [arXiv 2602.21611](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21611)
- [BODY] AWM asks for sub-task-level routines of at least two steps that recur across tasks; measured coverage of Mind2Web test examples by induced workflows was 0.40 — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)

### Inferences
- The failure evidence reconciles as follows: a failure is useful as the *input* to a lesson ("when X, do not Y, do Z") and harmful as a *stored example* to imitate. Systems whose retrieval path is imitation (Synapse, AWM) gain nothing from failures; systems that distil gain 3 points. A failure-only policy loses, probably because the store then holds prohibitions without any positive procedure.
- Raw versus distilled is a question of distance between the stored and the next task. Near-duplicate tasks (ALFWorld, repeated formats) reward verbatim reuse; shifted tasks (SWE-bench issues, AIME years) reward abstraction. A repository's recurring chores are the near-duplicate case; its bugs are the shifted case.
- Two independent results put the best quantity at one retrieved unit (ReasoningBank k=1; subtask memory top-1) and a third at one to three (SkillsBench). The designs that inject a growing list on every run are the ones with the documented cost problem.
- Meta-Harness and the Anthropic contract table say the same thing about the *analysis* step that Memp says about storage: summarising before the consumer has looked loses the detail that mattered. Distil for the acting agent, keep raw traces for whoever diagnoses.

### Gaps
- The ReasoningBank failure ablation is a single domain (WebArena-Shopping) and model; no variance is reported.
- No study read isolates failure-derived lessons on a coding benchmark.
- No study compares the same lesson delivered as prose versus as an executable check; all granularity results are about prose or code units given to the model.
- Memp's per-strategy numbers and the k curves in Memp and ExpeL are in figures and were not extracted.

## 3. Why does self-generated knowledge often fail to help?

### Takeaway
SkillsBench's body confirms the round-1 open question and strengthens it: self-generated skills were not merely unhelpful, they scored 8.1 to 11.5 points *below* no skills on all three configurations tested, while curated skills added 18.2 to 24.8 points on the same configurations. Across studies the causes are consistent: the generated artefact is never opened, it restates what the agent can already discover, it is admitted on an unreliable self-judgement and then imitated, and it costs tokens and steps on every run.

### Cited Findings

**SkillsBench (arXiv 2602.12670 v4, 14 June 2026; v1 13 February 2026)**
- [BODY] Conditions: no skills (task instruction only); curated skills (the task's skills directory); self-generated skills ("the agent first authors skill packs with Anthropic's skill-creator, then solves with only those packs"). 87 tasks, 8 domains, 18 model-harness configurations, three trials per cell — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- [BODY] Table 6, pass rate in percent (no skills / self-generated / curated): Claude Code + Opus 4.7 43.0 / 34.9 / 61.2 (self-generated −8.1, curated +18.2); Codex + GPT-5.5 46.8 / 35.5 / 66.5 (−11.3, +19.7); Gemini CLI + Gemini 3.1 Pro 36.0 / 24.5 / 60.8 (−11.5, +24.8). The paper states self-generated skills "land below the no-Skills baseline on all three configurations" — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- [BODY] Protocol (Appendix D.6): two isolated sessions. A creator agent receives the task package with curated skills removed and only the skill-creator mounted, and is told to author skills, not solve the task. A fresh solver then runs the task with the generated packs. For Claude Code the creator and solver run back to back in each trial's sandbox; for Codex and Gemini CLI packs are generated once and reused — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- [BODY] The authors' audit of 12 trajectories names three mechanisms: packs "the solver never discovers" (10 of 12 audited Codex and Gemini CLI trajectories never list, read or mention the generated skills); "creator-side authoring that displaces solver work"; and "confidently wrong pack content when the skills are used" (one pack asserted STL units were millimetres and hard-coded a 1000x conversion, so both solver trials were wrong by three orders of magnitude) — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- [BODY] The one clear win for self-generation was leakage: on one task the creator wrote the pack inside the graded sandbox and it named the exact components and test IDs the verifier checks; the authors call it an answer key, not a reusable procedure — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- [BODY] Caveats stated by the authors: the condition covers only 3 of 18 configurations and is "kept as a diagnostic", outside the main aggregate; Claude Code's self-generated runs used effort level max while its baseline used high; the deficit "mixes content quality with skill-discovery and creator/solver interference effects"; one verifier was patched after the runs; creator sessions for two harnesses are not in the released artefacts — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- [BODY] Even curated skills hurt on 13 of 87 tasks (largest drops −7.4 points). Software Engineering had the second-smallest curated gain of the eight domains (+11.6 pp; Natural Science +28.8) — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)
- Note for the report writer: the self-generated condition in SkillsBench is *pre-task authoring from the task description*, not distillation from a completed trajectory. It tests "can the model write itself useful guidance before trying", which is a weaker setting than learning from experience. The v4 body contains no figure of −1.3 pp or any reference to earlier-version findings.

**Context-file studies ("Evaluating AGENTS.md", ETH Zurich; arXiv 2602.11988 v3, 29 September 2026)** — replaces round 1's abstract-level entry
- [BODY] Benchmarks: SWE-bench Lite (300 tasks, 11 repositories, none with a developer context file) and a new benchmark of 138 instances from 12 repositories that do have developer-committed context files (mean 641 words, 9.7 sections). Naming conflict: the v3 body calls it CTXbench; round 1's secondary source called it AGENTbench — [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)
- [BODY] Success rate, none / LLM-generated: SWE-bench Lite Sonnet 4.5 59.9 / 58.7; GPT-5.2 56.6 / 54.4; GPT-5.1 mini 47.7 / 47.6; Qwen3-30B 31.1 / 32.4. CTXbench none / LLM-generated / developer-written: Sonnet 4.5 73.2 / 65.2 / 70.3; GPT-5.2 65.2 / 68.1 / 68.1; GPT-5.1 mini 54.3 / 50.7 / 55.8; Qwen3-30B 45.7 / 47.1 / 53.6 — [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)
- [BODY] Statistics: generated versus none is not significant on either benchmark (about −0.5 points, p = 0.87; about −2 points, p = 0.37). Developer-written versus none is about +2.4 points, p = 0.21. Developer-written versus generated is significant (p = 0.038, about 4.2 points). The cost increase is significant: about 20% and 23% for generated files, up to 19% for developer files; steps rise by 2.45 to 3.92 — [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)
- [BODY] Why: (1) overviews do not help the agent find the right files sooner; an LLM judge found a codebase overview in 95 to 100% of files generated by three of the four agents, and steps before first touching a relevant file did not fall; (2) the instructions *are* followed (a tool named in the file is used about 1.6 times per instance versus under 0.01 when not named), so agents run more tests and explore more; (3) reasoning tokens rise 14 to 22%; (4) the content is redundant with existing documentation: when all documentation was removed from the repositories, generated files improved performance by 2.7% on average and beat developer files — [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)
- [BODY] Ablations: a stronger generator model did not produce better files; file length had no clear effect; removing the testing section cut cost without changing accuracy. Recommendation: omit generated context files for now, and have human-written files contain only what is "not already present in the README", such as specific conventions — [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)

**Error propagation and experience-following (Xiong et al.; arXiv 2505.16067 v2, 10 October 2025)** — extends round 2's entry with the tables
- [BODY] Final performance and memory size by addition policy (fixed memory / add-all / LLM judge C1 / stronger LLM judge C2 / fine-tuned judge C3 / ground-truth oracle): EHRAgent accuracy 16.75 / 13.05 / 26.19 / 32.21 / 34.66 / 38.50; AgentDriver success 40.11 / 32.32 / 36.92 / 40.01 / 47.37 / 51.00; CIC-IoT accuracy 71.50 / 59.90 / 74.00 / 68.80 / 79.50 / 85.40 — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] Add-all is below a frozen memory on all four agents. An off-the-shelf LLM judge as gatekeeper is not reliably better than frozen memory (AgentDriver 36.92 vs 40.11; CIC-IoT C2 68.80 vs 71.50). A judge fine-tuned on 300 labelled trajectories beats frozen memory on three of four agents and ties on the synthetic one — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] Mechanism: high input similarity between the task and a retrieved record yields highly similar output, so a wrong stored output is replicated; the gap to an error-free memory appears immediately and widens over time — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- Venue note: the page read (v2) names no venue; round 1's "ACL 2026" label is unverified.

**Other bodies read that show the same causes**
- [BODY] ReasoningBank's admission judge is 72.7% accurate, and the store is never pruned — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)
- [BODY] ExpeL: insights written by a weaker model (gpt-3.5) were no better than hand-written generic ones (32.0 vs 32.0) and well below gpt-4's (39.0) — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)
- [BODY] Dynamic Cheatsheet reduced accuracy for a weaker model (GPT-4o-mini) and on knowledge-heavy tasks for GPT-4o, and gave little for reasoning models — [arXiv 2504.07952](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07952)
- [BODY] SkillWeaver's residual failures are selection failures: the right function exists and is not picked, or is called with wrong arguments — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)
- [BODY] AWM: workflow actions were invoked in only 18.5% of tasks, and workflows pushed the agent to follow a routine where it did not fit — [arXiv 2409.07429](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.07429)
- [BODY] Anthropic: an older model over-recorded (413 files) and spent 17 consecutive steps on memory without acting; injected memory "use[s] up valuable tokens" and longer instruction files reduce adherence — [Anthropic engineering, 6 Nov 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)
- [BODY] Subtask memory: half of all stored entries that were ever retrieved were retrieved exactly once (50.7% of 797) — [arXiv 2602.21611](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21611)

### Inferences
- Five separable causes, each with at least one body-level measurement: (1) **non-activation**: the artefact exists and is never loaded (SkillsBench 10 of 12; AWM 18.5%; SkillWeaver selection errors); (2) **redundancy**: it restates what the agent finds anyway (AGENTS.md documentation-removal experiment); (3) **unreliable admission**: a self-judge at roughly 73% accuracy, or a raw LLM gatekeeper that does not beat frozen memory, lets wrong content in; (4) **faithful imitation of wrong content**: experience-following and the millimetre example, made worse because agents do follow instructions; (5) **standing cost**: about 20% more spend and several more steps per task, and lower compliance as the injected text grows.
- Curated skills work for the mirror-image reasons: a human supplies information the model does not have and cannot discover in the sandbox (domain procedure, a non-standard convention), in a small unit, and somebody checked it. The AGENTS.md data say the same from the other side: developer files are significantly better than generated ones, and their content is the non-obvious convention.
- The implication for "prose that the agent wrote for itself" is that its expected value is near zero or negative unless it carries information the next run could not cheaply rediscover, and unless something other than the author vouched for it.
- A mechanical enforcer sidesteps causes (1), (4) and (5) by construction: a lint rule or hook fires without being read, costs no context, and cannot be half-followed. It does not sidestep (3): a wrong rule is still wrong, so admission has to be tested against real code (section 5).
- Weak-model sensitivity recurs (ExpeL, Dynamic Cheatsheet, SkillWeaver, round 1's "Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit"): the quality of the writer and the ability of the reader to use the artefact are separate constraints.

### Gaps
- SkillsBench does not test skills distilled from completed trajectories, so "self-generated from experience" versus "curated" has no direct controlled comparison in the sources read.
- No study measures non-activation rates for learned rules or skills in a production coding harness.
- The earlier-version SkillsBench figure recalled in round 1 could not be located; treat it as superseded by Table 6 of v4.
- The AGENTS.md study is Python-only and measures task resolution only; it does not measure whether convention-type instructions reduce review rework.
- No study read measures how much of the developer-file benefit comes from specific rule types.

## 4. What mechanisms keep a knowledge store correct, and which have measured support?

### Takeaway
Measured support exists for a gate on admission whose judge has been calibrated on labelled data, for deletion driven by downstream outcome after a minimum number of uses, for bounding the store, and for consolidation that checks notes against the raw record. Counters as bookkeeping, embedding deduplication, time-based expiry, contradiction detection and provenance fields are widely implemented but I found no ablation that isolates their effect on task success.

### Cited Findings

**Admission gates**
- [BODY] Gate quality decides the outcome: with the same pipeline, moving from add-all to an LLM judge to a judge fine-tuned on 300 trajectories to an oracle moved EHRAgent accuracy 13.05 → 26.19 → 34.66 → 38.50 — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] Voyager: removing the success check before adding a skill cut discovered items by 73% — [arXiv 2305.16291](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.16291)
- [BODY] SkillWeaver admits a function after static checks for known mistakes and an executed test; no ablation isolates this stage — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)

**Usage and outcome counters; utility-based deletion**
- [BODY] Xiong et al. define two deletion rules. Periodic: delete records retrieved at most α times in a window (α = 0 or 1; windows of 200 to 500 steps). History-based: delete a record once it has been retrieved more than n times and its average downstream utility is at or below a threshold (for example n = 5, threshold 0.5) — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] With an oracle evaluator, combining both rules shrank memory by about 70 to 79% with accuracy changes of −4.37 (synthetic regression agent), +3.67 (EHRAgent), −1.03 (AgentDriver) and +0.10 (CIC-IoT). History-based deletion alone changed accuracy by −1.15, +3.39, +0.81 and +4.20 — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] With a raw LLM judge supplying the utility signal, history-based deletion was mixed: EHRAgent 25.91 → 33.55, AgentDriver 36.92 → 34.00, CIC-IoT 74.00 → 73.70. The authors report fine-tuned evaluators give more stable results and that "future task evaluations can serve as free quality labels for stored memory" — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] Under a fixed capacity (100 or 180 records), applying periodic deletion and then evicting the lowest-utility record kept performance high; values are in figures only — [arXiv 2505.16067](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)
- [BODY] ExpeL's vote counter (start at 2, remove at 0) and ACE's helpful/harmful counters are implemented; neither paper ablates the counter. ACE's pages do not state that counters trigger pruning — [arXiv 2308.10144](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144); [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- [SECONDARY] A practitioner pattern page (last reviewed 30 October 2026) recommends retiring a promoted rule when its hit count trends to zero over many pull requests, attaching an explicit obsolescence condition to each rule, and treating frequent suppression comments as a sign the rule sits at the wrong layer; no measurement is offered — [agentpatterns.ai](https://agentpatterns.ai/code-review/review-feedback-to-rule-loop/)

**Deduplication and size bounds**
- [BODY] ACE's embedding dedup threshold has little effect across 50 to 90% (77.0, 73.9, 78.6), and capping context at 10K versus 100K tokens changes nothing (78.6 vs 78.3) — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)
- [BODY] Mem0 deduplicates at write time through its ADD/UPDATE/NOOP choice and stores about 7K tokens per conversation versus over 600K for Zep, which the authors attribute to redundant summaries — [arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)
- [BODY] ReasoningBank does no deduplication or pruning and its performance falls as more items are retrieved (49.7 at k=1 to 44.4 at k=4) — [arXiv 2509.25140](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)

**Contradiction handling**
- [BODY] Mem0 deletes a memory contradicted by a new fact; its graph variant marks the old relation invalid and keeps it for temporal reasoning. No ablation of either policy on its own — [arXiv 2504.19413](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.19413)
- [BODY] ACE lists contradiction detection as a possible extension only — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)

**Provenance and citation checks**
- [BODY] Generative Agents store each reflection with pointers to the memories cited as evidence — [arXiv 2304.03442](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442)
- [BODY] Anthropic's study reports models annotating their own notes with verification status: one marked facts as checked or unconfirmed, another recorded how it knew each rule was true; wiki pages link to sources. Dreaming checks notes against transcripts, which "gave Claude a ground truth to validate its memory against". The Pokémon run with corrected memory reached the next city at action 1,935; the run with uncorrected memory looped and backtracked; the run with no memory never arrived — [Anthropic engineering, 6 Nov 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)
- [BODY] SkillWeaver keeps a usage log on each function — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)

**Periodic consolidation**
- [BODY] Anthropic: 128 dreaming sessions raised wiki-only accuracy from 13% to 48%; a second pass repointed nine broken links and added three pages. Costs acknowledged: dreaming "requires compute", and checking every memory continuously "might not be practical"; the suggestion is to focus on information likely to change — [Anthropic engineering, 6 Nov 2026](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)
- [BODY, round 1] Counter-example: letting an LLM rewrite the whole store collapsed ACE's context from 18,282 to 122 tokens in one step. Dynamic Cheatsheet's curator showed the same tendency in miniature, replacing stored content with a placeholder — [arXiv 2510.04618](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618); [arXiv 2504.07952](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07952)
- [BODY] Letta's reorganisation step backs up the memory repository before splitting, merging or restructuring; the optional pre-apply review is another model, not a human — [Letta docs](https://docs.letta.com/configuration/memory)

**Human curation and staleness**
- [BODY] Human-written beats generated in both controlled comparisons: SkillsBench (curated +18.2 to +24.8 vs self-generated −8.1 to −11.5) and AGENTS.md (developer vs generated, p = 0.038) — [arXiv 2602.12670 v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670); [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)
- [BODY] Google's AutoCommenter learned best practices that later changed (for example Python import guidance); because retraining was slow the team suppressed stale rules with regex-based conditional filters. Suppressing 17 non-actionable rule URLs (plus 5 similar) and rewording summaries raised the developer "useful" ratio from about 54% to 66% and the independent-rater ratio from 60% to 74% — [arXiv 2405.13565](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13565)
- [BODY] The memory tool documentation recommends deleting files not accessed for a long time; no evidence is given — [Claude platform docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/tool-use/memory-tool)

### Inferences
- Ranked by evidence: (1) a calibrated admission gate; (2) outcome-based deletion with a minimum-use threshold; (3) a hard size bound with lowest-utility eviction; (4) consolidation that re-checks against the raw record instead of rewriting from memory; (5) human review. Items (1) to (3) rest on one paper across four agents; (4) on one vendor study; (5) on two controlled comparisons.
- The outcome signal used for deletion must itself be trustworthy: with a raw LLM judge, utility-based deletion made two of three agents worse. In a coding harness the available hard signals are test results, CI status, whether a check's finding was fixed or suppressed, and whether a human reverted the change.
- Counters need two numbers, not one: how often the item was applied, and what happened afterwards. Xiong's rule only fires after n uses, which prevents deleting a rule on one bad outcome. The suppress-rate signal from the practitioner pattern and AutoCommenter's per-rule suppression are the static-check analogue of a "harmful" counter.
- Deduplication and context caps look like hygiene, not levers: ACE's sensitivity tables are flat. What matters is how many items reach the model per task, not how many are stored.
- Keeping the raw transcript is what makes later correction possible. Stores that keep only the distilled item (ReasoningBank, Mem0 after DELETE) have nothing to re-verify against.

### Gaps
- No ablation was found that isolates helpful/harmful counters, time-based expiry, contradiction detection or provenance fields.
- No measurement of contradiction rates between learned rules in a long-lived rules file.
- Xiong et al. do not study merging, summarisation or reflection, by their own statement; their deletion results under capacity limits and distribution shift are in figures only.
- Anthropic's results are a vendor engineering post without a full methods section; CL-Bench task counts and variance were not given in the text read.
- No source measures staleness of learned knowledge after a codebase changes.

## 5. Is there research or practice on converting learned knowledge into executable checks instead of text?

### Takeaway
Yes, but it clusters in security: generating Semgrep or CodeQL rules from vulnerability patches, where the validation protocol is clear (the rule must flag the pre-fix code and stay silent on the post-fix code) and reported success is roughly half of attempts, with heavy filtering of what survives. Outside security, direct evidence for turning a review comment or an observed bug into a lint rule is thin and mostly practitioner-level; the strongest adjacent evidence is on compiling written standards into linter configuration and on generating bug-reproducing tests.

### Cited Findings

**Static-analysis rule synthesis from patches**
- [BODY] QLCoder (Wang, Li, Dutta, Naik; arXiv 2511.08462 v4, 25 March 2026): from a CVE description plus the vulnerable and patched commits, an agent writes a CodeQL query. A validator compiles and runs each candidate on both versions; success requires at least one reported path in the vulnerable version that touches the patched code and none in the fixed version. Failed candidates return with feedback, up to 10 iterations — [arXiv 2511.08462](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08462v4)
- [BODY] Results on 176 CVEs across 111 Java projects: 53.4% (94/176) of queries pass validation; 57.7% for pre-2025 CVEs and 46.2% for 2025 CVEs. On 130 shared CVEs: recall 80.0%, average precision 0.672, average F1 0.700, versus stock CodeQL 20.0% / 0.055 / 0.073 and IRIS 35.4% / 0.031 / 0.048. Plain Claude Code without the tooling reached 10%. Average 3,712 seconds and $2.90 per query — [arXiv 2511.08462](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08462v4)
- [BODY] Ablation on 20 CVEs: full system 55%; without the CodeQL language server 25%; without documentation and reference queries 20%; without the AST cache 25%. Inconsistency flag: overall F1 is 0.700 in one table and 0.658 in another — [arXiv 2511.08462](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08462v4)
- [SNIPPET] Autogrep (lambdasec, February 2025): LLM-generated Semgrep rules from vulnerability patches followed by a filter that removes duplicates and overly specific patterns; the author's write-up reports 39,931 patches yielding 645 rules across 20 languages (a 1.6% survival rate by my arithmetic). Author's own claim, no independent evaluation found — [Security Boulevard repost](https://securityboulevard.com/2025/02/autogrep-automated-generation-and-filtering-of-semgrep-rules-from-vulnerability-patches/)
- [SNIPPET] RuleRefiner (ASE 2025, "Fact Aligned and Template Constrained Static Analyzer Rule Enhancement with LLMs"): repairs existing Semgrep rules from false-positive and false-negative reports; up to 80.28% success on 218 real Semgrep rule issues (the conference page timed out) — [ASE 2025 page](https://conf.researchr.org/details/ase-2025/ase-2025-papers/180/Fact-Aligned-and-Template-Constrained-Static-Analyzer-Rule-Enhancement-with-LLMs)
- [SNIPPET] QLPro reports 90.90% syntactic correctness of generated CodeQL rules with a two-model setup versus 70.01% with a single model; Patch2QL (arXiv 2401.12443) generates CodeQL from patches to find cognate defects — [alphaxiv 2506.23644](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2506.23644); [arXiv 2401.12443](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12443)

**From natural-language standards to linter configuration**
- [SECONDARY] LintCFG, "Still Manual? Automated Linter Configuration via DSL-Based LLM Compilation of Coding Standards" (arXiv 2602.07783, February 2026): compiles a written standard through a rule DSL into Checkstyle XML or ESLint JSON, with an LLM alignment check against the original standard. Reported: DSL representation over 90% precision, recall and F1; Checkstyle generation for the Google Java style 72.1 to 73.5% accuracy, 69.3 to 81.3% precision, 70.3 to 82.4% recall; ESLint for Google JavaScript 71.1 to 75.2% accuracy, 75.6 to 84.4% precision, 63.0 to 70.4% recall; removing the alignment checker cost up to 10.1% precision. Figures are from a third-party summary, not the paper — [Moonlight summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/still-manual-automated-linter-configuration-via-dsl-based-llm-compilation-of-coding-standards)

**From review comments to enforcement**
- [BODY] AutoCommenter (Google; arXiv 2405.13565, 22 May 2024; AIware '24) **[background, pre-2025]** keeps the learned check inside a model, not as a rule: a T5-based model trained on about 800K historical review comments that link to a best-practice document, posting comments for 330 distinct documents (68% of historical human best-practice comments by coverage). Of the 50 most frequently predicted practices, 33 (66%) were judged beyond what traditional static analysis can detect — [arXiv 2405.13565](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13565)
- [BODY] Its validation before exposure is the most complete read here: temporally split validation and test sets; per-rule confidence thresholds (a single 0.98 threshold left about 80% of sub-threshold predictions correct, so it was replaced); a filter for comments on unchanged lines (80% of raw predictions landed on lines the change did not touch); staged rollout (authors, about 3,000 volunteers from July 2022, an A/B test on about half of developers from July 2023, general availability October 2023); an independent rater study (15 developers, about 370 comments); a target of 80% "useful". Estimated resolution rate of posted comments about 40%. The A/B test found no significant change in review duration — [arXiv 2405.13565](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13565)
- [BODY] Lesson stated by the authors: strong intrinsic (offline) results did not predict deployment success — [arXiv 2405.13565](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13565)
- [BODY, practitioner experiment, small n] Cursor forum post (Ofer Shapira, 25 October 2026): 15 merged Ruff pull requests, 45 human review comments, 2 candidate conventions that recurred across pull requests, 1 that survived testing on real code. The rejected rule was a real convention in one part of the codebase and wrong elsewhere. Author's conclusion: repetition in review text is "the start of a check, not automatic approval" — [Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/turning-repeated-code-review-comments-into-cursor-rules-a-small-experiment/176123)
- [SECONDARY] The "review-feedback-to-rule loop" pattern: promote a comment after it appears on three or more pull requests; choose "the cheapest mechanism that fires deterministically" (style → linter rule; architectural boundary → AST or import check; safety → type or runtime check; contract → integration test); write the remediation text before the check; warns that "a semantic check forced into a regex linter produces false positives". No measurements of its own — [agentpatterns.ai, reviewed 30 Oct 2026](https://agentpatterns.ai/code-review/review-feedback-to-rule-loop/)
- [SNIPPET] The first search found no peer-reviewed study that converts code review comments into static-analysis rules with an LLM; MLinter (arXiv 2301.10082) is a pre-LLM feasibility study on learning ESLint rules from examples — [MLinter](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.10082)

**Tests as the executable form of a learning**
- [SNIPPET] Generating a test that fails before the fix and passes after it ("fail-to-pass") from an issue report: LIBRO 33% on 750 issues at 50 samples, 19.9% with one; Otter 31.4%, Otter++ 37.0%; best model on TDD-Bench Verified 23.6%; a follow-up (e-Otter++) reports 63% on TDD-Bench Verified; an industrial study at Google on 80 internal bugs reports that giving generated reproduction tests to an automated repair system fixed 30% more bugs. Rates are not comparable across benchmarks and sampling budgets — [TDD-Bench Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02883v1); [Otter](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05368v1); [arXiv 2502.01821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01821v2); [arXiv 2508.06365](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06365v3)
- [BODY] SkillWeaver is a non-coding example of the same idea: a learned skill is kept only after a generated test of it runs — [arXiv 2504.07079](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07079)

**Specification mining**
- [SNIPPET] "Improving Dynamic Specification Inference with LLM-Generated Counterexamples" (arXiv 2604.10761; ICST 2026): LLM-written tests that try to break inferred assertions discard up to 11.68% of the invalid assertions SpecFuzzer produces; a secondary summary gives precision 67.83% → 74.17% with recall unchanged across 43 subjects — [ICST 2026 page](https://conf.researchr.org/details/icst-2026/icst-2026-research/36/Improving-Dynamic-Specification-Inference-with-LLM-Generated-Counterexamples)
- [SNIPPET] A 2025 study of LLM-generated formal specifications lists syntax errors, incorrect postconditions and faulty loop invariants as common failures — [arXiv 2503.04779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.04779v2)

### Inferences
- The validation protocol that transfers directly to a coding harness is QLCoder's differential test: a generated check is admissible only if it fires on the code as it was before the fix and is silent on the code after it. A harness that has the bug-introducing and bug-fixing commits has both sides for free.
- That differential test proves the check catches *this* instance; it says nothing about false positives elsewhere. The Cursor experiment (a rule true in one directory, wrong in the rest) and Autogrep's 1.6% survival rate show the second gate is the binding one: run the candidate over the whole repository at a known-good commit and count findings before enabling it.
- Expect roughly half of synthesis attempts to fail validation even with tool support (53.4%), and expect tool support (language server, reference rules) to matter more than the model (55% versus 10 to 25% in the ablation). A harness should treat "no valid check could be produced" as a normal outcome and fall back to a test or to prose.
- Not everything is mechanisable. Google's own categorisation put two thirds of the most common review-time practices beyond static analysis. The realistic split for a learning is: lint or AST rule where the pattern is syntactic; a regression test where the failure is behavioural; a hook or gate where it is procedural; and prose only for the remainder, which the AGENTS.md study says should be limited to non-obvious conventions.
- AutoCommenter's deployment sequence is the only read example of an end-to-end acceptance process for learned checks: calibrate per rule, restrict to changed lines, stage the rollout, measure "useful" ratings and resolution, and keep a per-rule kill switch. Its finding that offline scores did not predict acceptance argues for measuring a new check in report-only mode before it blocks anything.
- A regression test is the cheapest executable form with the best-understood validation (fail before, pass after), but single-shot generation succeeds only about 20 to 37% of the time in the snippet-level figures, so it needs the same retry-and-validate loop.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study was found that converts review comments, CI failures or agent mistakes into lint or AST rules and reports precision and false-positive rates. This is the central gap for the harness design.
- No published accuracy figures were found for LLM-generated ast-grep or custom ESLint rules specifically.
- No vendor write-up (Semgrep, GitHub/CodeQL, Sonar) on LLM-generated rules was read in this session; Semgrep's assistant features are not covered.
- RuleRefiner, Autogrep, QLPro, the test-generation studies and the specification-mining studies are snippet-level; LintCFG is from a third-party summary. Their numbers need checking against the papers before quotation.
- QLCoder is security-only and Java-only; its false-positive behaviour on unrelated repositories is not reported in the text read.
- "Rules as code" in the legal or policy sense, and LLM-generated runtime guardrails for agents, were not researched.

## 6. What is known about mining code review comments, CI failures and version-control history as sources of learnings for coding agents?

### Takeaway
There is a growing empirical literature describing review comments, CI results and rejection reasons for agent-authored pull requests, and two systems that mine human issue history into agent memory, but I found no study that closes the loop from a mined signal to a validated rule and then measures the effect on later agent work. Reverts and fix-up commits as a learning source appear to be unstudied.

### Cited Findings
- [SNIPPET] "Where Do AI Coding Agents Fail?" (arXiv 2601.15195; MSR 2026 context): studies about 33K agent-authored pull requests from five coding agents across merge outcomes, CI results and review dynamics; code-level reasons account for 22% of rejections, dominated by CI or test failures (17%, 99 pull requests) — [arXiv 2601.15195](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15195)
- [SNIPPET] The AIDev dataset (arXiv 2602.09185) includes 19,450 inline review comments with file-level context and 88,576 commits linked to pull requests; a companion study builds a 12-category taxonomy of review themes over 19,450 comments from 3,177 agent pull requests and tests whether LLMs can annotate them reliably — [arXiv 2602.09185](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2602.09185.md); [arXiv 2601.19287](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19287)
- [SNIPPET] A study of AI fix-related pull requests reports that agents generally produce compilable code but fail at validation: build failures are rare and test failures common — [emergentmind summary of arXiv 2602.00164](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2602.00164.md)
- [SNIPPET] "Why Are Agentic Pull Requests Merged or Rejected?" (arXiv 2605.22534, 2026) warns that rejections may stem from agent failure, workflow constraints or undocumented reviewer decisions, and that these must be separated before attributing a rejection to agent capability — [arXiv 2605.22534](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.22534.md)
- [ABSTRACT] MemGovern mines human issue-resolution history from GitHub into 135K governed experience cards and reports +4.65% SWE-bench Verified resolution — [arXiv 2601.06789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06789)
- [BODY] AutoCommenter is the one deployed system trained on review history: about 800K comment examples, restricted to comments that cite a best-practice document, which gives every learned check a human-written rationale to point to. It found review history contains obsolete guidance that must be suppressed — [arXiv 2405.13565](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13565)
- [BODY, small n] In the Cursor forum experiment the yield from review history was low: 45 comments on 15 pull requests gave 2 recurring conventions and 1 usable rule — [Cursor forum, 25 Oct 2026](https://forum.cursor.com/t/turning-repeated-code-review-comments-into-cursor-rules-a-small-experiment/176123)
- [BODY] The AGENTS.md study found that what helped in developer-written files was information not otherwise in the repository, such as specific conventions and non-standard tooling, and that named tools are reliably used once mentioned — [arXiv 2602.11988 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)
- [SECONDARY, unverified] A practitioner page attributes to a Replit post of 27 October 2026 a workspace-memory feature that proposes skill updates for reviewer approval, with internal claims of 51% lower token use and one task cut from 16 minutes to 1. A search did not find the Replit post, and the page itself notes no run count or task breadth is given; do not cite the figures as established — [agentpatterns.ai](https://agentpatterns.ai/code-review/review-feedback-to-rule-loop/)
- [SNIPPET] A 2025 ACL workshop paper distils successful SWE-bench trajectories into repository-level insights in the ExpeL style; not opened — [ACL Anthology preview](https://preview.aclanthology.org/landing_page/2025.realm-1.30.pdf)

### Inferences
- The signals rank by how mechanically they can be labelled. A CI or test failure is an unambiguous outcome with a log attached. A revert or fix-up commit gives a before and after pair, which is exactly the input a differential rule validator needs (section 5). A review comment needs interpretation, is often local to one part of the codebase, and may be stale.
- Review comments are a low-yield, high-noise source: one usable rule from 45 comments in the small experiment, and Google needed per-rule thresholds, suppression lists and a filter for unchanged lines to reach acceptable usefulness. Requiring recurrence across several pull requests and anchoring each candidate to a written rationale are the two filters both sources converge on.
- The rejection-reason caveat applies to any harness mining its own history: a rejected or reverted change is not always evidence of an agent mistake, so the miner needs the reason, not just the event.
- Because test failures dominate code-level rejections of agent pull requests, the highest-value learnings for a coding agent are likely about validation behaviour (which tests to run, how, before claiming done), and those are enforceable as gates instead of advice.

### Gaps
- No study was found that mines reverts, fix-up commits or `git blame` history to derive rules or checks for coding agents.
- No study measures the downstream effect of rules mined from review comments on later agent pull requests (merge rate, review rounds, CI pass rate).
- All MSR-style findings in this section are snippet-level; taxonomy categories and per-agent rates were not read from the papers.
- The Replit workspace-memory claim could not be traced to a primary source.
- No source read addresses mining CI logs specifically (as opposed to CI pass or fail status) into reusable knowledge.
