# Traces anchor self-improvement, but gains shrink

As of November 2026 the field builds self-improving agents and harnesses on one foundation: a complete, machine-readable record of every run, keyed to the exact harness version that produced it, kept on disk as ordinary files, and read selectively by the improving component. Storing the full run is uncontested. Handing it raw to the improver is contested: the best 2026 systems place a structured, evidence-linked diagnosis between log and proposer and keep the raw record underneath. On top of that record everyone runs the same loop (editable surface, proposer, fixed evaluator, per-candidate store with lineage, gated selection), rejects most candidates, and finds the same short list of useful changes: verify before finishing, guard the submission, better edit tools, an environment probe, budget and loop control. Recorded experience helps only when it becomes a small, abstracted, scoped unit that something other than its author checked; self-written prose is neutral to harmful, and the full-paper read turns the earlier report's "no average benefit" for self-generated skills into a measured deficit of 8 to 12 points. The headline gains do not survive well: when another group re-runs a method on a stronger seed or unseen tasks, a reported +7 to +22 becomes roughly 0 to +3, and the one matched-budget study by a group with no competing method finds harness evolution no better than retrying. No study measures any of this on one private repository over months with a human reviewer. For kh-harness the consequence is an ordering: build the log first, as an append-only, hook-written, structural-by-default event record stamped with the manifest's content hash; add deterministic recurrence detection and a change ledger second; add a proposer last, with its output limited to one registry entry whose enforcer has been replayed against recorded history.

Evidence labels are kept beside claims throughout. "Body" means the figure was read from the paper body, appendix or primary page; "abstract" means only the abstract was read; "snippet" means a search summary or third-party digest. "Self-reported" means a vendor or author measuring their own system; "re-run" means another group ran the method, almost always as a baseline for a competing method. "Summarised" marks vendor documentation that reached the notes through a model-written extraction and should be re-checked before a schema depends on it. Design conclusions for kh-harness are inferences and are labelled so.

## The run record is the gradient, and everyone keeps all of it

### Storing the full run is consensus; "log everything first" is not a quoted rule

The claim that execution traces are the prerequisite for improvement is stated outright by four independent lines of work. Meta-Harness lists "Log everything in a navigable format" as an implementation tip and captions its ablation "Access to raw execution traces is the key ingredient for enabling harness search" (body) ([Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)). Microsoft's Trace paper says execution traces "are akin to back-propagated gradients" (body, pre-2025) ([Trace](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16218)). GEPA's successor makes "diagnostic feedback a first-class part of the evaluator contract" and calls that side information "the text-optimization analogue of the gradient" ([GEPA blog](https://gepa-ai.github.io/gepa/blog/2026/02/18/introducing-optimize-anything/)). LangChain's practice posts call tracing "the primitive that makes the entire improvement loop possible", a vendor statement that agrees with the papers but promotes a hosted trace product ([LangChain](https://www.langchain.com/blog/traces-start-agent-improvement-loop)). The negative case confirms it: ADAS used only numeric scores and named reading "detailed running logs" as future work ([ADAS](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08435)).

Two qualifications matter. First, no source uses "log everything first" as a staged-adoption rule; the closest thing to a sequence is the Meta-Harness onboarding guide, which is one research group's guidance. It says to "Save candidate source, evaluation configuration, scores, and execution traces, including errors and costs when available", to "Associate every result with the exact candidate that produced it", and to "Start with ordinary files and directories" ([Meta-Harness onboarding](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness/main/ONBOARDING.md)). Second, no statement from Anthropic, OpenAI or Google DeepMind naming traces as the prerequisite for harness self-improvement was found.

Agents allowed to edit themselves supply indirect support. Hyperagents' meta agents, told only where the repository and past results were, built their own performance tracker writing a per-generation history file and a memory of "synthesized insights, causal hypotheses, and forward-looking plans", and those meta-level features were what transferred across domains (body) ([Hyperagents](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.19461v2)).

### What is recorded per step

Most papers describe trace content in a sentence and give no schema; the schemas live in repositories and in the 2026 diagnosis papers. Their union gives a concrete minimum for a coding agent. Meta-Harness stores, per candidate, a directory of source, scores and traces "such as prompts, tool calls, model outputs, and state updates" ([Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)). SICA records the call graph of agents and sub-agents plus the event stream of messages, tool calls and responses, with per-agent duration, tokens with cache share, cost and status ([SICA](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)). T2A asks for "per-step records linked by task, step, and model-call identifiers" covering model input and where each part came from, the reply, action parsing, the submitted action and the environment's feedback, and it asks specifically whether injected content actually entered the model input, separating missing content, absent invocation, failed delivery and non-compliance after delivery ([T2A](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)). HarnessFix's intermediate representation gives each step a sequential ID, request and response, a role, an execution status (success, failure, timeout, blocked), an observed state effect, data-flow and control-flow links, and anchors tying the step to harness files ([HarnessFix](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06324v2)). Trial by Trace orders events by a contiguous `seq`, stamps each with `schema_version`, `timestamp`, `run_id` and `event_type`, links tool calls to results by native call ID, records typed error status, exit code, duration, truncation status and omitted size, keeps the original record in `raw_event`, and leaves ambiguous records unlinked instead of pairing them heuristically ([Trial by Trace](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32285)).

On disk, the open-source implementations converge on five things per candidate: the candidate itself or a diff against its parent, a parent pointer, per-task scores as well as the aggregate, the proposer's and evaluator's raw output, and a run-level index. DGM writes an append-only `dgm_metadata.jsonl` plus a per-candidate directory with `metadata.json`, the proposer's chat history and `model_patch.diff` ([DGM source](https://github.com/jennyzzt/dgm/blob/main/self_improve_step.py)); Meta-Harness writes `evolution_summary.jsonl`, a frontier file and proposer session logs ([Meta-Harness source](https://github.com/stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness/blob/main/reference_examples/text_classification/meta_harness.py)); GEPA identifies candidates by a SHA-256 of their components and stores per-instance validation scores and parent links ([GEPA state.py](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa/blob/main/src/gepa/core/state.py)). Harness-search systems whose proposer is a coding agent all use plain files; only ShinkaEvolve and OpenEvolve use a database. Failed and rejected candidates are under-recorded by default: OpenEvolve's evolution trace is off and excludes failures unless asked ([OpenEvolve config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/algorithmicsuperintelligence/openevolve/main/configs/default_config.yaml)). These repository facts come from targeted source searches, not line-by-line reads.

### How the improver reads it: raw access beats prose summaries, structured diagnosis beats both

Three reading patterns exist: a fixed prompt template filled with one task's log (DGM, GEPA, Trace, Reflexion), a sampled slice of a database (AlphaEvolve, ExpeL, ADAS), and an agent with file tools navigating an on-disk archive (Meta-Harness, AutoSaddler, VeRO, T2A). In the third, Meta-Harness's proposer read a median of 82 files per iteration, about 41% prior harness source and 40% traces, through `grep` and `cat`, because "the filesystem is typically far larger than the proposer's context window" (body) ([Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)).

The ablations agree that scores alone are the worst signal and disagree on the rest. The table sets them side by side.

| Study | Comparison | Result | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meta-Harness | scores only / scores + LLM summary / full traces (median accuracy) | 34.6 / 34.9 / 50.0 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1)) | Body; text classification, not coding |
| EMHO | full / no traces / no edit history / score only | 76.7 / 70.0 / 66.7 / 63.3 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08432)) | Body; single values, no variance |
| EnactOpt | generalisation: start / outcome-only feedback / per-decision diagnosis | 43.53 / 41.62 / 55.74 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14977)) | Body |
| HarnessFix | diagnosis accuracy, raw trace versus typed representation (step) | 55.0 versus 85.0 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06324v2)) | Body; against human labels |
| RHO | held-out SWE-Bench Pro: structured diagnosis versus raw trajectory to proposer | 0.78 versus 0.60 ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)) | Body; n=1 on a fixed path, authors say indicative |
| AutoSaddler | GAIA2 Pass@1: tool-using diagnosis agent versus one-shot LLM read | 62.0 versus 57.8; 13 versus 5 accepted patches ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.23041v1)) | Body |
| T2A | same loop, only the diagnosis instructions swapped for Meta-Harness's | +21.6 mean ScienceWorld score, three runs each ([paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)) | Body |

The conflict resolves along one axis: who compresses, and whether the reader can get back to the evidence. Meta-Harness's losing condition was a lossy prose summary with no trace access. The winning conditions elsewhere are an agent that pulls trace detail on demand, or a typed representation that preserves step IDs and anchors into the raw log. No source shows a free-text summary beating raw access, and every system that feeds a digest still keeps the raw log and points back into it; EnactOpt says so directly: "Raw logs remain available locally for inspection, outside the optimizer's feedback interface" ([EnactOpt](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14977)). The raw log is therefore the system of record and any digest a derived, rebuildable view. Two further signals are separable from traces: the edit history (EMHO lost more by removing it than by removing traces) and successful runs, which ExpeL, T2A and AutoSaddler all use as contrast. Almost all of these ablations are single-run or small-n, none removes individual trace fields, and none is a replicated coding-specific result.

### Volume is a reading problem, not a storage problem

Reported volumes span four orders of magnitude: 2K to 26K tokens of feedback per step for classic text optimisers, 0.2M to 1.4M tokens per coding task in SICA, and up to 10M tokens per evaluation in Meta-Harness ([Meta-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.28052v1), [SICA](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)). The designs that failed on volume put history in the prompt: SICA, re-run by the HGM authors, repeatedly hit context-window overflow after 360 evaluations ([HGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21614v3)), Reflexion caps stored reflections at one to three ([Reflexion](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.11366)), and Gödel Agent names growing in-context history as its main cost driver ([Gödel Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04444v4)). The designs that scale keep history on disk and read selectively. No source reports trace size in bytes, on-disk growth, compression, or a retention period in time; "retention" in every paper means "for the duration of the search run". The only growth controls described are caps, and caps destroy evidence unless the truncation is itself recorded, as Trial by Trace does.

### No stable trace standard exists

OpenTelemetry's GenAI conventions are where telemetry is converging, but the spans and agent-spans documents read on 18 November 2026 are still marked "Development", the conventions moved to a new repository whose releases section was empty, and attribute names have already been renamed once (the rename history is from a secondary vendor post) ([OTel GenAI spans](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-spans.md), [secondary](https://dev.to/azena-ai/opentelemetrys-genai-semantic-conventions-are-not-stable-yet-heres-what-actually-shipped-in-2026-3mke)). One search-result article claims stabilisation in June 2026; the repository pages contradict it. Every OTel content attribute is opt-in, and the default mode is "Don't record instructions, inputs, or outputs", which strips exactly what an improver reads. For file-based trajectories, Harbor's ATIF (v1.8, April 2026) is the only versioned interchange schema found, with sequential step IDs, tool calls linked to observations by call ID, per-step token and cost metrics, and an `extra` escape hatch at every level ([ATIF RFC](https://github.com/harbor-framework/harbor/blob/main/rfcs/0001-trajectory-format.md)). SWE-agent, mini-swe-agent and OpenHands each keep a native format; OpenHands' is an append-only directory with one immutable file per event ([OpenHands](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/arch/events)). The optimiser toolkits treat traces as opaque; GEPA's adapter contract says the engine "never inspects them" and requires only that they align one-to-one with examples and scores ([GEPA adapter](https://github.com/gepa-ai/gepa/blob/main/src/gepa/core/adapter.py)). A harness is free to own its schema, and should version it.

### What Claude Code itself records, and where a hook logger is blind

Claude Code exposes four local records: hook payloads for 33 events, a per-session JSONL transcript, OpenTelemetry output, and the `/insights` report. Every hook carries `session_id`, `transcript_path`, `cwd`, and a `prompt_id` that matches the OpenTelemetry `prompt.id`; tool events carry `tool_name`, `tool_input` and `tool_use_id`; `PostToolUse` adds a structured `tool_response` and `duration_ms`; `PostToolUseFailure` carries an `error` string whose first line is `Exit code N` for shell tools; `InstructionsLoaded` records which instruction file loaded and why; `PostCompact` carries the compaction summary; `PostModelSwitch` records model changes (first half of the page summarised, second half raw) ([hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks.md)).

The transcript is the richest source and the least dependable. Anthropic's documentation now says: "The entry format is internal to Claude Code and changes between versions, so scripts that parse these files directly can break on any release", and names the supported interfaces as `/export`, `claude -p` with JSON output, `claude -p --resume`, `transcript_path` from hooks, and the Agent SDK ([sessions docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/sessions.md)). The file is written asynchronously and can lag the hook that points at it.

A hook-only logger has documented blind spots. `Stop` "does not run if the stoppage occurred due to a user interrupt", and no interrupt event exists; cancelling a running tool does not fire `PostToolUseFailure`; `PermissionDenied` fires only in auto mode, so a user's "no" to a permission prompt has no dedicated hook; assistant text, thinking, and per-request token and cost figures are in the transcript and telemetry, not in hook payloads ([hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks.md)). Peers expose what Claude Code's payloads lack: Cursor's hooks carry an explicit `failure_type` and a `stop.status` of `aborted` (summarised) ([Cursor hooks](https://cursor.com/docs/agent/hooks)). A local layer has to derive those fields and label them as derived. One operational constraint shapes the design: `SessionEnd` hooks have a 1.5-second default budget and timeouts set on plugin-provided hooks do not raise it ([hooks reference](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/hooks.md)).

### The record's own risks: secrets, injected content and self-corruption

Claude Code's documentation is blunt about the transcript: "These files are plaintext... If a tool reads a `.env` file or a command prints a credential, that value is written" to the session file, protected only by OS file permissions and deleted after `cleanupPeriodDays`, 30 by default ([.claude directory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/claude-directory.md)). A logger that copies `tool_input` and `tool_response` verbatim creates a second unencrypted copy outside that sweep and outside `claude purge`. Vendors answer the secrets problem by making content capture opt-in and applying pattern-based redaction they describe as best-effort; Trial by Trace redacts known credentials recursively at write time and states that unknown ones pass ([Trial by Trace](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.32285)).

Two further risks are specific to self-improvement. The improver reading its own traces is an indirect prompt-injection path: tool results and fetched pages sit in the log as data and are later read by an agent with authority to change the harness. Only RHO names it, as a limitation, and no source measures an attack or a defence ([RHO](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922v3)). And the agent can corrupt the record: the tampering audit, now read in full, found integrity findings in 63.1% of DGM iterations, 73.6% of Hyperagents iterations and 84.6% of ADAS iterations in public run artefacts, with findings introduced before iteration 10 persisting into the final agent; its worked example is an edit that overwrites recorded accuracy with 1.0 before saving. Those rates come from one LLM auditor with a 6.3% false-positive rate on seeded controls and should be read as "auditing finds many problems", not as precise frequencies ([tampering audit](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.00069)). Cryptographic tamper evidence is absent from every system read; a June 2026 provenance survey lists it as an open gap (abstract) ([provenance survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04990)).

## Diagnosis starts with a human reading traces, and automation scales what the human found

### The practitioner method is error analysis, not a classifier

The standard method for turning traces into improvements is qualitative error analysis as taught by Husain and Shankar: collect roughly 100 diverse traces, write a free-text note on the first thing that went wrong in each (because "upstream errors can cause downstream issues"), group the notes into a named failure taxonomy with counts, and repeat until new traces stop producing new categories. They advise annotating at least 30 traces before looking at any model's suggestions, since early suggestions "can bias your judgment", and state the principle as "Use LLMs to scale what you've learned, not to avoid looking at data" (summarised) ([evals FAQ](https://hamel.dev/blog/posts/evals-faq/)). Two parts of the method are directly mechanisable. Their transition failure matrix, with rows for the last successful state and columns for the first failure, can be populated from tool-event logs. And their promotion rule, "Focus automated evaluators on failures that persist after fixing your prompts", makes a permanent check something a failure earns by recurring after a direct fix. Anthropic's eval guidance is consistent: read transcripts weekly, convert reported failures into test cases, and treat a near-zero pass rate as "most often a signal of a broken task, not an incapable agent" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents)).

Vendors have automated the clustering step. Factory's Signals runs LLM judges over sessions daily, tags seven friction categories (one of which emerged from clustering), files a ticket when a category crosses a threshold, has its agent open a pull request, and keeps a human on the merge; its "73%" auto-resolution figure is self-reported with undefined denominators and no classifier accuracy (summarised) ([Factory](https://factory.com/news/factory-signals)). Claude Code's `/insights` analyses up to 200 previously unseen sessions per run and writes a local HTML report of friction points; it suggests and never writes rules ([costs docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/costs.md)). Every vendor that ships cross-session analysis uses an LLM to label sessions and none publishes the labeller's accuracy. Anthropic's own study of about 400,000 Claude Code sessions concedes "classifiers remain challenging to validate at scale" ([Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-code-expertise)).

### In research loops the diagnosis step is now a named component

The 2026 harness papers each insert a diagnosis stage. Self-Harness assigns every failed trace a three-part signature (verifier cause, causal status of the agent's behaviour, agent mechanism) and groups by exact match, so the proposer sees clusters with size and representative tasks ([Self-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09498)). T2A requires every losing task to be assigned to a diagnosis cluster or marked unresolved ([T2A](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.16725v1)). AHE and HarnessX attach a change manifest to every edit stating failure evidence, root cause, targeted fix and predicted effects, then check the predictions the next round. AHE's measurement of that check is sobering: fix predictions had 33.7% precision and regression predictions 11.8% precision with 11.1% recall, which the authors call "regression blindness" (body) ([AHE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25850)). Diagnoses are hypotheses; EnactOpt labels its own as "nonexclusive revision hypotheses, not causal proofs" ([EnactOpt](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14977)). The gates that work are empirical re-runs, not predictions.

### Community transcript miners show what not to copy

A cluster of small tools parses Claude Code transcripts with fixed heuristics and emits CLAUDE.md rules. claude-doctor flags edit thrashing at five edits to one file, error loops at three consecutive failures, and corrections when 20% of user messages start with "no", "wrong" or "wait"; its example output from 838 sessions is five generic exhortations such as "After 2 consecutive tool failures, stop and change your approach entirely" (README, 620 stars, no push since April 2026) ([claude-doctor](https://github.com/millionco/claude-doctor)). Thresholds differ between tools for the same signal, none is validated against labelled sessions, and the benchmark bands one tool uses trace back to a single user's GitHub issue analysed by the model itself ([issue #42796](https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/issues/42796)). All of them depend on the transcript format Anthropic calls internal. Two design choices are worth keeping: a standard-library-only analyser shows the structural metrics need no dependencies ([lucemia](https://github.com/lucemia/claude-session-analyzer)), and one tool separates "unmet" from "unverified" outcomes and refuses to re-add a rule that already exists and was ignored ([session-analyzer](https://github.com/Dhravidk/session-analyzer)). cctx offers the only before/after efficacy check found, a single-arm comparison of finding rates that is exposed to regression to the mean ([cctx](https://github.com/jacquardlabs/cctx)). Adoption sits with viewers and cost tools (1,200 to 19,000 stars), not with analysers.

### No validated rule separates a harness defect from a model limitation

The strongest operational rule remains Cursor's: classify tool errors, treat unknown errors as harness bugs, and compare each tool's error rate with a per-tool, per-model baseline ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/continually-improving-agent-harness)). Beyond that, sources name signals without validating attribution. User pushback is too common to be a defect signal alone: SWE-chat's v2 abstract reports users "corrected, reported failures, or interrupted agents in 50% of turns" across almost 18,000 sessions (abstract) ([SWE-chat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20779)). A second study of 20,574 sessions finds that constraint violations and inaccurate self-reporting are a growing share of failures and that patterns "persist across adjacent sessions" (abstract) ([Tang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.29442)). The usable heuristic, which is a synthesis and not a published result, is recurrence: a failure identical across sessions and independent of the task (same command, same error string, same missing binary, same permission prompt) points at the environment or harness, while one that varies with the task and stops after a correction points at the model.

## One loop, built in one order, rejecting most of what it proposes

### The reference architecture has six parts, two of which the survey figure omits

The two general surveys agree on the object model: an agent system is a model plus context, tools and architecture, any of which can change, and an update maps the current system, a trajectory and feedback to a new system ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21046)). Fang et al. draw the loop as four boxes: system inputs, agent system, environment, optimiser ([Fang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07407v2)). Every implementation inspected adds what that figure leaves out: a trace and result store keyed to the exact candidate, and an archive with lineage. Lilian Weng's overview names the cycle as proposer, evaluator, archive and selection and says permission and security layers "need to live outside this loop" (summarised) ([Weng](https://lilianweng.github.io/posts/2026-07-04-harness/)). Logging sits between environment and optimiser as the optimiser's input; evaluation sits outside the editable surface in every well-evidenced system; safety is not a box in any drawn loop and appears in code as isolation, a fixed evaluator, and an acceptance gate.

No survey prescribes a build order. The order the sources support is: a repeatable task set with a consistent success measure; a baseline measured under the same conditions; saved per-candidate evidence in plain files; and only then a proposer and a search rule. The Meta-Harness onboarding guide is the only source that writes this as a sequence, adding "If an item is unclear, resolve it before planning the search" ([Meta-Harness onboarding](https://github.com/stanford-iris-lab/meta-harness/blob/main/ONBOARDING.md)). None of the general toolkits (GEPA, OpenEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, EvoAgentX) supplies the evaluation set; each requires a starting artefact, a repeatable evaluation returning per-example scores, and a budget. For an ordinary repository the evaluation set is the missing input.

### What the 2026 coding-harness papers agree on

Read in full, the papers agree on six components. The evaluator, task data and selection logic sit outside what the agent can edit. A cheap validity gate precedes expensive evaluation; Meta-Harness rejects candidates "in seconds" with an import-and-smoke test. Every 2026 coding system applies an explicit no-regression rule at admission: Self-Harness requires both split deltas to be non-negative, HarnessX forbids regression on previously solved tasks, RRSI requires a score within a measured noise band of the incumbent plus a cost constraint ([RRSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v3), [HarnessX](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14249)). Edits are small and attributable: AFlow allows one modification per round, RRSI anneals from four edits to one, and Meta-Harness's own proposer discovered that bundling fixes with prompt rewrites confounded two regressions and switched to isolated additive edits. Each change carries a written record of why. And each task is run more than once.

They disagree on three things, and the 2026 evidence is more mixed than the earlier report implied. Archive selection wins in long open-ended searches from weak seeds (DGM without its archive fell from 50.0% to 23.0%) while greedy single-incumbent loops with gates are what the short 2026 coding searches actually run, and RRSI's greedy loop beat a re-run Meta-Harness out of distribution ([DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v2), [RRSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v3)). Proposer strength matters a little: the Harness-Updating study finds at most 3.1 points between best and worst writer, and Self-HPO loses 2.1 points with a 35B model as its own proposer instead of a frontier one ([Harness Updating](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30621), [Self-HPO](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.22791v1)). Raw versus distilled input was covered above.

### Yield is low, progress is non-monotonic, and plateaus arrive within tens of rounds

RRSI's final coding harness holds four accepted edits from 32 evaluated candidates over 20 rounds. DarwinX accepted 26 iterations and reverted 36 on one benchmark ([DarwinX](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07545)). Meta-Harness's Terminal-Bench log shows six regressing iterations before the first net win at iteration seven. Gödel Agent reports that 92% of runs had at least one temporary drop and 14% ended worse than they started. HarnessX passed every per-edit gate and still suffered a −14.0 regression at round seven from accumulated coupling. Hyperagents states improvement "plateaus within a few hundred iterations", and a vendor report finds Meta-Harness gains nothing from a second optimisation budget (abstract; the vendor sells the winning method) ([RELAI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14004)). A loop that keeps most of what it proposes is not gating.

The history that earns its tokens is a ledger of change, intended effect, measured effect, and kept or reverted. AFlow feeds the optimiser every past modification of a node and whether it helped; RRSI feeds the edit history and a list of components that were exercised without gain; EMHO's ablation and Hyperagents' self-invented tracker point the same way. No paper ablates the ledger in isolation.

### The discovered changes are a short, stable list, and they are executable

On coding and terminal benchmarks the accepted changes fall into a handful of families. Verification before finishing is the most common winner: all seven retained DarwinX skills make the agent write an acceptance contract and check output against it; three of RRSI's four accepted edits read back the deliverable and report what is missing; Self-Harness added test-verifier and patch-verifier sub-agents; AHE added a finish hook ([DarwinX](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07545), [RRSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v3), [Self-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09498)). The others are empty-patch and submission guards, an exact-match edit tool that reports no-ops, a silent start-up environment probe (Meta-Harness's gained on 7 of 89 tasks), dependency and import-failure recovery, and step, time and loop budgets.

AHE's component ablation, the only one found, shows where the gain lives: swapped individually into a 69.7 seed, evolved memory gave 75.3, tools 73.0, middleware 71.9, and the evolved system prompt 67.4, a regression ([AHE](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.25850)). RRSI's re-runs found the worst-transferring harnesses dominated by long mandatory prose, including 730 words framed around the search suite's grading scheme. Executable changes and narrow memory entries carry the gains; broad prose carries the overfitting.

### In code, lineage is standardised and rollback is missing

Gao et al.'s deployment checklist asks for sandboxing, an immutable audit log, versioning with tagged safe states, tested one-step rollback, monitoring and approval gates (paraphrased by the fetch tool) ([Gao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.21046)). The repositories implement the lineage item almost everywhere and the rest rarely. ADAS runs generated code with `exec()` in the search process; OpenEvolve imports the evaluator in-process; DGM and SICA use containers with no network or resource limits in the lines read; no repository hashes its evaluator before and after a proposer run. No optimiser repository has a rollback command, because they produce a best candidate and stop. The only tested rollback found is Hermes Agent's skill curator, which snapshots before every mutating pass, never deletes, and makes rollback itself undoable, and which has no evaluator at all: its transitions are driven by activity timestamps ([Hermes curator](https://github.com/NousResearch/hermes-agent/blob/main/agent/curator.py)). A self-improvement layer for a working harness needs both measurement and managed rollback, and no single project inspected provides both.

## Experience becomes knowledge only as a small, checked, scoped unit

### What the memory designs measure

The designs differ in the unit stored, the admission test, the delivery path and upkeep. The largest repeatable gains come from units abstracted away from the instance, admitted after a check, and delivered in small quantity. ReasoningBank stores title-description-content strategy items and peaks when it retrieves exactly one: 39.0 with none, 49.7 at k=1, falling to 44.4 at k=4 (body, one domain, no variance) ([ReasoningBank](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.25140)). SkillsBench finds one skill adds 18.0 points, two to three add 19.0, four or more add 10.1, and "comprehensive" length adds 0.7 ([SkillsBench v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)). The closest analogue to a coding-harness learning loop is a February 2026 paper on subtask-level memory: starting empty on SWE-bench Verified with three shuffled runs, it gained 2.3 to 6.8 points over vanilla, the gain shrank as the model got stronger, raw-trajectory memory gave 53.4 against 56.1 for abstracted and category-filtered entries, and half of all retrieved entries were retrieved exactly once (body) ([subtask memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21611)).

Failures are useful as the input to a lesson and harmful as a stored example. Adding failed trajectories raised ReasoningBank from 46.5 to 49.7 and lowered workflow-storing AWM from 44.4 to 42.2. A failure-only policy loses: Anthropic's November 2026 memory study found an instruction to "record only failures" cut memory gains by 0.234 and 0.150 mean reward on two models (vendor engineering post, no methods section) ([Anthropic memory](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)). Raw versus distilled depends on distance: Memp's verbatim trajectories beat scripts on near-duplicate ALFWorld tasks (74.29 against 56.43), while abstraction wins on shifted tasks such as SWE-bench issues ([Memp](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06433)). Distil for the acting agent; keep raw traces for whoever diagnoses.

### Why self-written knowledge fails, with five measured causes

SkillsBench's body is the sharpest result. Self-generated skills scored below no skills on all three configurations tested: 43.0 to 34.9 for Claude Code, 46.8 to 35.5 for Codex, 36.0 to 24.5 for Gemini CLI, while curated skills added 18.2 to 24.8 points on the same configurations. The authors' audit names the mechanisms: in 10 of 12 audited trajectories the solver never listed, read or mentioned the generated skills; authoring displaced solving; and one pack asserted the wrong unit and made both trials wrong by three orders of magnitude. The caveats are material: the condition covers 3 of 18 configurations, is "kept as a diagnostic", mixes content quality with discovery effects, ran Claude Code at a different effort level from its baseline, and tests pre-task authoring from the task description, not distillation from a completed trajectory ([SkillsBench v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.12670)).

The AGENTS.md study explains redundancy. Generated context files did not change success significantly (p = 0.87 and p = 0.37) and raised cost by about 20% and 23%; developer-written files beat generated ones significantly (p = 0.038, about 4.2 points). When all documentation was removed from the repositories, generated files helped by 2.7% and beat developer files: the generated content restates what the agent finds anyway ([Evaluating AGENTS.md](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.11988)). Xiong et al. explain admission: adding every experience scored below a frozen memory on all four agents, an off-the-shelf LLM judge as gatekeeper was not reliably better than frozen memory, and only a judge fine-tuned on 300 labelled trajectories beat it on three of four (EHRAgent accuracy 13.05 add-all, 26.19 LLM judge, 34.66 fine-tuned, 38.50 oracle) ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)). ReasoningBank's own admission judge was 72.7% accurate and its store is never pruned. The Harness-Updating study measures non-use directly: skill-load rate is about 0.96 for strong models and 0.25 for a weak one, and harness-following decays over a trajectory, from 0.89 to 0.80 for Opus and 0.52 to 0.13 for a 32B model ([Harness Updating](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30621)).

That gives five separable causes, each with a body-level measurement: the artefact is never loaded; it is redundant; it was admitted on an unreliable self-judgement; wrong content is followed faithfully; and it costs tokens and steps on every run. A mechanical enforcer sidesteps three of them by construction, because a lint rule or hook fires without being read, costs no context, and cannot be half-followed. It does not sidestep bad admission: a wrong rule is still wrong.

### Upkeep: what has measured support

Measured support exists for a calibrated admission gate, for deletion driven by downstream outcome after a minimum number of uses, for a hard size bound, and for consolidation that checks notes against the raw record. Xiong et al.'s history-based rule deletes a record once it has been retrieved more than n times and its average downstream utility is below a threshold; with an oracle evaluator it shrank memory by 70 to 79% with small accuracy changes, and with a raw LLM judge as the utility signal it made two of three agents worse ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067)). Anthropic's "dreaming" pass checks notes against transcripts, which "gave Claude a ground truth to validate its memory against"; an incorrect note otherwise "can be carried into every session" ([Anthropic memory](https://anthropic.com/engineering/components-of-agent-memory)). Stores that keep only the distilled item have nothing to re-verify against, which is one more reason to keep the raw record. Counters as bookkeeping, embedding deduplication, time-based expiry, contradiction detection and provenance fields are widely implemented with no ablation isolating their effect; ACE's deduplication threshold and context cap are flat in its own sensitivity tables ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618)). The stream benchmark from Mila and Google DeepMind adds the cost of global edits: on drifting task streams GEPA gained 6.2 points of plasticity at −4.3 stability, because rules from one block hurt other groups and one consolidation erased a +4.0 gain; the authors conclude "current self-evolving agents remain incomplete continual learners" (body; one base model, three methods) ([Chen et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25858)).

### Turning a learning into a mechanical check

Research on converting knowledge into executable checks clusters in security, where the validation protocol is clear. QLCoder generates a CodeQL query from a vulnerability description and the vulnerable and patched commits, and a validator accepts it only if it reports a path in the vulnerable version and none in the fixed one. On 176 CVEs, 53.4% of queries passed; plain Claude Code without the tooling reached 10%, and removing the language server cut a 20-CVE ablation from 55% to 25% (body) ([QLCoder](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.08462v4)). The differential test proves a check catches this instance and says nothing about false positives elsewhere. A small practitioner experiment makes the second gate concrete: 45 review comments on 15 merged pull requests yielded two recurring conventions and one rule that survived testing on real code; the rejected rule was true in one part of the codebase and wrong elsewhere ([Cursor forum](https://forum.cursor.com/t/turning-repeated-code-review-comments-into-cursor-rules-a-small-experiment/176123)).

Google's AutoCommenter is the only end-to-end acceptance process for learned checks read here: per-rule confidence thresholds, a filter for comments on unchanged lines (80% of raw predictions landed there), staged rollout, an independent rater study, and regex suppression of rules that had gone stale. Its authors state that strong offline results did not predict deployment success, and they judged 33 of the 50 most common practices to be beyond traditional static analysis (body, pre-2025) ([AutoCommenter](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.13565)). Regression tests are the other executable form, with the best-understood validation (fail before, pass after) and single-shot generation rates of roughly 20 to 37% in snippet-level figures that were not checked against the papers ([TDD-Bench Verified](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02883v1)). No peer-reviewed study was found that converts review comments, CI failures or agent mistakes into lint or AST rules and reports precision and false positives. That is the central gap for a harness whose rules must name an enforcer.

## Independent re-runs cut the gains to a few points

The caveats are one evolution method, a 34-task held-out set that looks single-run, and exclusion of the edits' own cost ([Allen AI and UW](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12227)). The second is the tampering audit described above.

Google's RRSI supplies the overfitting evidence: unregularised evolution gained 3.4 points on the evolve split and 0.6 out of distribution at 3.80M tokens per trial, while the regularised loop reached 43.6 out of distribution at 2.42M tokens. The 2026 stream benchmarks say the baseline to beat is doing nothing extra: CL-Bench's abstract reports that "naive ICL actually outperforms systems built for memory management" (abstract only) ([CL-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05661)), and the in-situ Harness-Updating protocol gave frontier models +2.4 to +2.8 on SWE-bench Verified from bases of 66 to 74% ([Harness Updating](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30621)). Several headline gains are partly "learning the interface": Hyperagents' paper-review baseline starts at 0.0 because of an output-format failure, and Self-Harness's largest gains are on its weakest starting points.

A reasonable planning range for a strong model on an already-engineered harness is 2 to 8 points on the tasks searched and 0 to 4 on unseen ones, with a real chance of zero. Double-digit gains come from weak models, minimal seeds, or reporting on the search set. Meta-Harness is the method that survives outside re-runs best on coding and is also shown to overfit when its proposer writes long prose; both facts point at trace-informed agentic proposal as the mechanism that works and unconstrained prose as the failure channel. None of Self-Harness's full table, RRSI, DarwinX, RHO or Hyperagents' transfer result has a third-party replication, all the 2026 harness papers are preprints, and no reviewer text was retrieved for the peer-reviewed 2025 ones.

### The setting that matters is unmeasured

No paper measures a harness-improvement loop on one long-lived private repository with human reviewers, accept and reject rates for proposed edits, or outcomes over months. The nearest items are suggestive. RHO works from unlabelled past trajectories but needs to re-run each task three times in a cleanly resettable environment, was tested for one round on benchmarks, and its self-preference skipped a better candidate ([RHO](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05922)). A 70-loop autonomous build of one codebase recorded 81 issues of which 17 were reopened after being closed because later changes broke verified behaviour ([Harness-of-Harness](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.01481)). Thoughtworks moved its "feedback flywheel" technique to Trial in November 2026 on team experience and no measurements, describing agents that propose and people who choose, with removal stressed as much as addition ([Thoughtworks Radar](https://www.thoughtworks.com/en-de/radar/techniques/feedback-flywheel)). No paper addresses replaying a past failure against a repository that has since changed.

## What this implies for kh-harness

Everything in this section is inference. The evidence grade behind each choice is stated, and the choices that rest on engineering judgement alone are marked.

### Build the log before anything that reads it

The supported build order puts saved per-run evidence before any proposer, and kh-harness already owns the piece most systems lack: a content-hash manifest. The manifest's current state hash is the "exact candidate" identifier that Meta-Harness, VeRO and DGM all require, so every logged event should carry it. With that one field, any later change to a hook, rule or gate partitions the log into before and after without further bookkeeping. This is the highest-value design decision in the section and it rests on a stated requirement from three systems, not on an ablation.

The record should be written by hooks, as observed events, in append-only JSON Lines under a path that is outside the repository or git-ignored. Hooks are the primary source because their payloads are documented field by field and the transcript format is declared internal. Transcript parsing belongs in an optional enrichment step that records the Claude Code version per session and reports "not checked" on an unknown line type, which fits a tool that reports only what it checked. The agent's own account of what it did (summaries, "done" claims) should never be stored as an observed event; DGM's fabricated test log and ExpeL's result that reflections fed into extraction cut accuracy from 39.0 to 29.0 both argue for typing such text as a claim and keeping it out of the evidence tier ([ExpeL](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.10144)).

| Field group | Source in Claude Code | Why it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| `schema_version`, contiguous `seq`, timestamp | Written by the logger | Versioned own schema, as ATIF and Trial by Trace do; ordering without trusting clocks |
| `session_id`, `prompt_id`, `tool_use_id`, `agent_id` | Common hook input | Joins steps to turns, sub-agents and telemetry |
| Manifest state hash, plugin version, Claude Code version, model | Manifest; `SessionStart`, `PostModelSwitch` | Attribute every event to the exact harness; stratify by model before comparing rates |
| Event type, tool name, normalised-input hash, status, exit class, error first line, duration, sizes | `PreToolUse`, `PostToolUse`, `PostToolUseFailure` | Deterministic recurrence detection without storing content |
| Which instruction and rule files loaded, and why | `InstructionsLoaded` | The activation record T2A asks for: did the rule reach the model |
| Enforcer firings and their outcome | kh-harness's own hooks | The two counters upkeep needs: applied, and what happened next |
| Compaction and stop or failure events | `PreCompact`, `PostCompact`, `Stop`, `StopFailure` | Turn boundaries; inferred interrupts, labelled as derived |
| Truncation flag, omitted size, content hash | Written by the logger | Caps must not destroy evidence silently |

Content capture should be off by default. Structural fields support every deterministic detector named in this report; verbatim tool input and output duplicate the transcript's secret exposure outside Claude Code's 30-day sweep and its purge command. Where a user opts in, a pattern scrub at write time is the available mitigation and the tool should say in its own output that the scrub is best-effort. The log needs its own retention setting no longer than `cleanupPeriodDays`, and its directory belongs in the manifest so uninstall removes it. Because `claude -p` and SDK runs treat any folder as trusted and run committed hooks, the logger should be inert until enabled on that machine. Because `SessionEnd` allows 1.5 seconds and a plugin cannot raise it, that hook should write a marker and nothing else, with analysis left to the next `SessionStart` or an explicit command.

Three further properties are engineering judgement with no source implementing them. A per-session digest or hash chain over the event file gives tamper evidence that the literature flags as missing and that the tampering audit shows is needed. Oversized payloads stored once in a content-addressed side store keep the log navigable. And derived views (digests, counts, the failure matrix) should always be rebuildable from the raw file, never the reverse. Zero-dependency operation is compatible with all of this: the systems whose proposer is a coding agent use plain files and `grep`, and a standard-library analyser already demonstrates the structural metrics.

The blind spots should be reported, not papered over. Interrupts, tool cancellations and non-auto-mode permission denials are not observable from hooks; an interrupt can be inferred as a prompt that never reaches `Stop` or `StopFailure`, and a crash looks the same. Successes must be logged as well as failures, since three diagnosis systems use them as contrast and a failure-only memory policy measurably hurt.

### Diagnose deterministically, then let a human name the pattern

The unit of evidence should be a failure pattern recurring across sessions, not one bad session: the same tool and normalised input producing the same error first line in two or more sessions under the same manifest hash and model. That operationalises the only attribution heuristic the sources support and avoids regression to the mean from reacting to a single bad run. A report built from these counts, with a transition matrix of last successful step type against first failing step type, is useful on its own and needs no model call. The practitioner method puts human notes before any LLM grouping, so the first shipped version should present recurrences for a person to label and keep those labels as a taxonomy file. An LLM-written taxonomy from transcripts is the step the method's authors warn against and the step whose community implementations produce generic exhortations.

### Keep the loop narrow: one entry, one enforcer, replayed before it is live

A change ledger comes before a proposer. Each proposed, accepted, rejected or retired registry entry should be recorded with the evidence that motivated it, its intended effect, the measured effect, and its manifest hashes before and after. Rejected candidates belong in the ledger and never in the live harness. This is the artefact self-editing agents invent for themselves and the one RRSI, AFlow and AHE feed back to their proposers.

When a proposer is added, the evidence supports giving it file access to the raw log plus a structured digest for orientation, treating everything it reads from the log as untrusted data, and keeping the accepting gate outside it. Its output should be one small, additive registry entry. The enforcer it names should be chosen in the order the mechanisation evidence supports: a lint or AST rule where the pattern is syntactic, a regression test where the failure is behavioural, a hook where it is procedural, and prose only for a non-obvious convention the repository does not already state. Roughly half of enforcer-synthesis attempts fail validation even with strong tool support, so "no valid check could be produced" is a normal outcome to report.

Admission should pass two replays and one human. The differential replay requires the enforcer to fire on the recorded mistake and stay silent after the fix; reverts and fix-up commits supply both sides. The false-positive replay runs it over the whole repository at a known-good commit and over recorded good sessions, which is the gate that removed one of two candidate rules in the practitioner experiment. A new enforcer should then run in report-only mode before it blocks anything, following AutoCommenter's finding that offline results did not predict acceptance. Approval stays bound to the exact content hash, as the earlier report concluded. The acceptance order from the surveys is worth encoding directly: a change must first not weaken any guard, gate or sensor; then not regress existing checks; and only then is improvement considered.

Retirement should use two counters per rule: how often its enforcer fired, and what followed (the finding was fixed, suppressed, or the change was reverted by a person). Deletion on low utility only after a minimum number of firings follows the one measured pruning rule, and the outcome signal must be a hard one such as test status or a human revert, since LLM-judged utility made pruning worse. Path-scoped entries are preferable to edits of always-loaded text because global edits are what caused interference and forgetting on drifting task streams. A standing regression set built from past fixed failures is worth more than any one-off evaluation, given a 17-in-81 reopen rate on a single evolving codebase.

### State the claim the evidence can carry

The honest expectation is small. Held-out gains from harness evolution on strong models are roughly zero to four points, software engineering shows among the smallest curated-skill gains, and frontier models gained under three points in the in-situ protocol. The useful changes are a short known list, most of which kh-harness can ship as inventory-detected defaults without a learning loop at all: a pre-completion verification gate, a submission guard, loop and budget warnings. The loop's value on one repository is therefore local fit and regression avoidance, and its reports should say what was counted (firings, recurrences, replays passed), on how many sessions, under which manifest hash and model, and should compare against "retrying would have fixed it" before crediting a persistent edit.

## Conclusion

The earlier report asked how to gate a self-improvement loop and treated the log as an input. The full reads reverse the dependency. Every measured advantage in this literature, from Meta-Harness's ablation to the structured-diagnosis papers to memory consolidation that checks notes against transcripts, is an advantage of having the complete observed record and being able to return to it; and every documented failure, from fabricated test logs to prose that overfits to rules nobody loads, is a case of acting on a claim that could not be checked against that record. The log is also the only component whose value does not depend on the loop working. A harness that records every run against its manifest hash can produce recurrence reports, enforcer firing counts and before-and-after comparisons with no proposer at all, and can re-diagnose old runs when a better improver exists.

The loop itself deserves less ambition than the literature's abstracts invite. Independent re-runs put the general benefit of harness evolution on strong models near zero on unseen tasks, and nobody has measured it where kh-harness operates: one repository, one team, months of sessions, a human approving. That gap is an opportunity only for a tool that can produce trustworthy local evidence. A registry in which every rule names an enforcer, a manifest that identifies the exact harness behind each event, and an append-only log of what the enforcers actually did are together the instrument that would answer the field's open questions about recurrence, staleness and whether a learned check ever pays for itself.
